# Community Organization

After the disaster, **the scarcest resource is not water: it is trust**. An organized neighborhood survives on little; one in conflict wastes even abundance.

## Critical steps

1. **First short assembly and TODAY:** 20 minutes maximum, standing, all households invited. Three points: what there is (inventory), what is urgent (water/health/latrine), who takes each task. It is recorded publicly.
2. **Roles by SKILL, not by shouting:** water (community guide), health (whoever knows + first-aid kits + this app), sanitation, census of vulnerable people, night security, communication (mesh + board). Each role with a backup.
3. **The board is the truth:** a physical point with: list of people (OK/sought), water rules, shifts, next assembly, needs/offers. It kills rumor, which is enemy #2.
4. **Resource rules BEFORE conflict:** water measure per person, line with a list, common food cooked together goes further, and donations are distributed publicly — distribution behind closed doors breaks trust in one day.
5. **Daily assembly of 15 min** (same time, same place): what was done, what is missing, relief shifts. Short and standing: endless assemblies kill participation.
6. **Conflicts: when calm and with witnesses.** Two people who clash do not negotiate by shouting in the line: it is written down, and resolved in the assembly or with 2-3 respected neighbors. Public golden rule: whoever attacks loses the turn, not the ration (punishing with hunger escalates violence).
7. **Connect outward:** the mesh between neighborhoods multiplies capacity (barter, doctors, tactical map alerts); a written census ready speeds things up with authorities/NGOs when they arrive — the documented neighborhood receives first.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Wait for "someone" to organize | Anyone calls the first assembly: you |
| One boss who decides everything | Distributed roles with backups and a public board |
| Discreet distribution "to avoid fights" | All distribution in view: opacity IS the fight |
| Assemblies lasting 3 hours | 15-20 min standing, daily |
| Punish conflicts by taking away food/water | Shifts and tasks yes; vital ration never |
| Ignore older adults, children, and sick people | Census of vulnerable people with a person responsible for each household |

## The organizer’s kit

- Board/wall + charcoal or marker.
- Census notebook: people per household, medical needs, skills.
- This app’s mesh: neighborhood channel + meeting point + tactical map.
- Assembly whistle (3 blasts = meeting).

## Practical example: Community Organization

**Situation:** Day 2 after the flood; 30 families in the school, donated food arrives mixed together and two neighbors almost fight over blankets.

**Do:** 1. Call with the whistle: 20 min assembly in the courtyard. 2. Public inventory on the board and roles: water, common kitchen, census, latrine, night. 3. Donations are sorted and distributed IN VIEW by family list. 4. The near-fight: it is resolved with 3 neighbors calmly after the assembly — blankets by census of children/older adults first. 5. Daily assembly 7 a.m. and the shelter mesh channel.

**Avoid:** Do not let "the strongest" manage donations, do not distribute at night in secret, do not hold marathon assemblies, do not leave the census "for later."

**Escalate:** If the conflict escalates to violence or someone hoards with threats: security in pairs (never one alone), it is documented in the notebook, the resource is isolated under rotating custody of 3 people, and a bridge with authorities is sought through the mesh — the neighborhood’s strength is that EVERYONE sees everything.
