# Critical first aid: MARCH/XABCDE algorithm

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care or hands-on training. It is for the first minutes, with limited resources, while you activate help or transport.

## Absolute priority

In a real emergency, do not try to “do everything.” Work in this order:

```text
0. Scene safety: do not become another victim.
1. Massive bleeding: spurting blood or clothing soaking through.
2. Airway: can air get in and out?
3. Breathing: normal breathing? open chest wound?
4. Circulation / shock: cold skin, weak pulse, confusion.
5. Disability: unresponsive, seizure, head injury.
6. Exposure + temperature: find hidden injuries and prevent cold/heat injury.
```

This is a layperson-safe version of MARCH/XABCDE used in prehospital trauma, combined with public first-aid principles.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Better |
|---|---|
| Treat small ugly wounds first | First treat what kills in minutes: bleeding, airway, breathing |
| Move a possible neck/back injury without need | Move only for fire, collapse, traffic, water, or no breathing |
| Give water, alcohol, or pills to a seriously injured person | Nothing by mouth if confused, vomiting, traumatised, or likely to need surgery |
| Use wire/thin cord as a tourniquet | Use 4-5 cm wide cloth or a commercial tourniquet |
| Pull out embedded objects | Pad and stabilize around them; they may be plugging bleeding |

## Step 0 — Safety and command

1. Look for 3 seconds: traffic, wires, gas, fire, weapons, collapse, water, smoke.
2. Use gloves if available; otherwise plastic bags over hands. If nothing is available, act anyway: life first.
3. Point to one person: “YOU, call emergency services.” To another: “YOU, bring first-aid kit/AED/cloth/light.”
4. If alone, call on speaker and work while talking.

## Step 1 — Massive bleeding

Act if blood spurts, pulses, soaks clothing, or pools on the ground.

1. Press hard with gauze, a clean shirt, or your hand directly on the bleeding point.
2. Deep wound in groin/armpit/neck: pack cloth/gauze into the cavity until full, then press on top.
3. Arm or leg bleeding that is not controlled quickly, pressure cannot be maintained, or amputation: tourniquet 5-8 cm above the wound, not over a joint. Tighten until bleeding stops completely and write the time.
4. Do not loosen the tourniquet outside a hospital.

## Step 2 — Airway

1. If they talk or cry, the airway is open.
2. If unresponsive: look in the mouth. Remove only visible objects; no blind finger sweeps.
3. No spine concern: gentle head-tilt/chin-lift.
4. Possible neck/back injury: jaw thrust if trained; if they do not breathe, opening the airway and CPR outrank spine protection.

## Step 3 — Breathing

1. Not breathing normally or only gasping: CPR.
2. Open chest wound that sucks air: cover with plastic/bag/wrapper taped on 3 sides so air can escape but not enter. If breathing worsens, briefly lift an edge.
3. Loosen tight clothing; use a comfortable position if awake and no spine trauma.

## Step 4 — Shock

Signs: cold pale sweaty skin, fast pulse, thirst, confusion, weakness.

1. Lay them down.
2. Keep warm above and below: the ground steals heat.
3. Raise legs only if there is no pain, fracture, pelvis/spine/head trauma, or breathing difficulty.
4. Nothing by mouth.
5. Urgent transport.

## Step 5 — Head, spine, seizures

- Hard blow, fall, crash, neck/back pain, weakness/tingling: hold the head in the position found and log-roll only if essential.
- Seizure: protect from injury, time it, put nothing in the mouth. Side position afterward if breathing.
- Unresponsive but breathing and no spine concern: recovery position.

## Step 6 — Exposure and hypothermia prevention

1. Look for hidden injuries without unnecessary undressing.
2. Cover again quickly. Trauma plus cold worsens bleeding and shock.
3. Use blanket, jacket, cardboard, backpack, or padding under the person.

## Safe improvisation

| Need | Improvise with | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure | shirt, towel, clean diaper, direct hand | removing soaked layers |
| Tourniquet | wide cloth + rigid stick | wire, shoelace, thin cord |
| Splint | cardboard, board, straight branch, rolled magazine | trying to set bones |
| Spine support | towels/clothes beside the head | pulling the neck or sitting them up |
| Chest seal | plastic bag/wrapper + tape | airtight 4-sided seal if breathing worsens |
| Warmth | blanket, jacket, plastic, cardboard under body | leaving them on cold ground |

## Evidence base

Based on public AHA 2025 CPR/AED principles, STOP THE BLEED/ACS bleeding control, ATLS 2025 and PHTLS/NAEMT trauma assessment concepts, and IFRC/Red Cross first aid. It does not copy proprietary courses; it translates priorities into layperson-safe actions.

## Example: Critical first aid: MARCH/XABCDE algorithm

**Situation:** In a remote area, someone has a serious injury and evacuation will take more than 30 minutes.

**Do:** 1. Make the scene safe and assign one specific person to call emergency services. 2. Follow the large steps in this guide in order instead of jumping to the most dramatic action. 3. Use gloves, barriers, or clean cloth if available. 4. Recheck every minute: breathing, responsiveness, bleeding, temperature, or worsening signs.

**Avoid:** Do not improvise advanced procedures, do not move the neck/spine if injury is suspected, do not give food or drink to a confused person, and do not delay calling because you are searching for perfect equipment.

**Escalate:** Call or evacuate immediately if breathing is not normal, chest pain appears, bleeding does not stop, confusion worsens, a burn is large, poisoning is possible, pregnancy is advanced, or there are multiple victims.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who is responsible next. This is a guided practice example; adapt it to your environment and prioritize professional help when available.
