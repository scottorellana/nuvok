# Emergency childbirth

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care. Get medical help as soon as possible.

Good news: **the vast majority of births go well on their own.** Your job is NOT to "pull the baby out" — it's to receive it, keep it warm and stay out of the way.

## When there's NO time to transport

- Contractions every 2 minutes or less, very strong.
- The mother has an **uncontrollable urge to PUSH** or "to use the toilet".
- **You can see the head** (crowning).
- In doubt with a far hospital: set up where you are. Delivering in a car halfway is worse.

## What NOT to do

- ❌ DON'T pull the baby — not by the head, not by the shoulders. It comes out with the contractions.
- ❌ DON'T pull the cord to "deliver the placenta".
- ❌ DON'T cut the cord without CLEAN tools: the baby can stay attached to the placenta for hours safely — wrap them together.
- ❌ DON'T cross the mother's legs or try to "hold back" the birth.
- ❌ DON'T bathe the newborn (deadly heat loss); just rub dry.

## Prepare (5 minutes)

- Wash your hands to the elbows; gloves if any.
- CLEAN, dry towels/clothes (several), a blanket for the baby, a hat or clean sock for its head.
- A flat spot with something clean under the mother; semi-sitting with knees bent and open, or on her side, or on all fours — WHATEVER POSITION SHE WANTS.
- If available: clean string/shoelace (ideally boiled) and boiled/flamed scissors or blade in case you'll cut the cord.

## Steps — the birth

1. **Encourage, don't shout:** push WITH each contraction, rest and pant between them.
2. **When the head appears:** place your hand GENTLY on it — only so it doesn't pop out abruptly. Ask her to pant (not push) while the head crowns.
3. Head is out: **check the neck.** Cord wrapped around? Slide it gently over the head, or loosen it so the body can pass through. Never yank.
4. Next contraction delivers shoulders and body — **the baby is SLIPPERY:** receive it firmly with both hands over a towel.
5. **Write down the time.**

## Steps — the newborn

1. **DRY IT NOW, rubbing firmly** with a towel, back included — rubbing stimulates crying. Crying = breathing.
2. Switch to a DRY towel, place **skin-to-skin on the mother's chest**, cover both, hat on the baby.
3. **Not crying/breathing?** Rub the back vigorously, flick the foot soles, 30-60 seconds. Still nothing? → baby CPR (guide "CPR for children and babies": 5 gentle initial breaths).
4. **Cord:** with clean material: wait 1-3 minutes (until it stops pulsing), tie TIGHT 4 finger-widths from the belly, tie again 2 fingers further, cut BETWEEN the ties. No clean material: DON'T cut; wrap placenta with baby.
5. **Breastfeed as soon as possible** — it makes the womb clamp down and stop bleeding.

## Steps — placenta and mother

1. The placenta delivers itself in 5-30 minutes. Catch it in a bag — **take it to the hospital** (they must check it's complete).
2. **Massage the womb:** rub firmly with your fist below the navel until you feel a "hard ball" — that stops bleeding. Repeat when it softens.
3. Bleeding like a heavy period is normal. **NOT normal:** a steady stream, more than 2 cups, or a pale/dizzy mother → continuous massage, baby to the breast, legs raised and URGENT transport.

## When to get help / transport — ALWAYS

Every emergency birth goes to the hospital afterwards (mother + baby + placenta). URGENT if: heavy bleeding, placenta not out within 1 hour, baby not gaining color/tone, breech (bottom first) or an arm/the cord comes first (mother chest-down, hips raised, and GO).

## Example practice: Emergency childbirth (the baby is coming now)

**Situation:** A pregnant person has very frequent contractions, strong pressure, and cannot reach the hospital in time.

**Do:** 1. Make the scene safe and assign one specific person to call emergency services. 2. Follow the large steps in this guide in order instead of jumping to the most dramatic action. 3. Use gloves, barriers, or clean cloth if available. 4. Recheck every minute: breathing, responsiveness, bleeding, temperature, or worsening signs.

**Avoid:** Do not improvise advanced procedures, do not move the neck/spine if injury is suspected, do not give food or drink to a confused person, and do not delay calling because you are searching for perfect equipment.

**Escalate:** Call or evacuate immediately if breathing is not normal, chest pain appears, bleeding does not stop, confusion worsens, a burn is large, poisoning is possible, pregnancy is advanced, or there are multiple victims.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who is responsible next. This is a guided practice example; adapt it to your environment and prioritize professional help when available.
