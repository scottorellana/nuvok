# The First 72 Hours

After the impact, the 72 hours begin when **the community IS the government**: outside help takes time — what you do in an organized way is worth a hundred improvisations.

## Critical steps

1. **Hour 0-1: people.** Family count (mesh meeting point), injured people with the guides (triage if there are several), and DO NOT re-enter damaged structures (collapse guide).
2. **Hour 1-6: hazards outside.** Gas shut off, wires marked at 10 m, at-risk structures marked with red cloth — a neighborhood hazard map on the mesh tactical map.
3. **Hour 6-12: honest inventory.** By family and in common: water, food, medicines, tools, generators, and SKILLS (nurse, mason, electrician). What is written is not disputed.
4. **Hour 12-24: water and sanitation first.** Secure a treated water source (community water guide) and emergency latrines BEFORE need improvises (sanitation guide) — the epidemic starts on day 3 if not.
5. **Day 2: roles and shifts.** Simple coordination (organization guide): water, health, night security, census of vulnerable people (older adults, babies, chronically ill people with their medicines counted).
6. **Day 2-3: communication and signals.** The mesh connects the neighborhood without signal; a physical information point (board/wall) with lists of people OK, sought, and needs — reduces panic and rumors.
7. **Golden rule: stabilize first, then rebuild.** No repairing roofs on day 1 while safe water is lacking; the order of the first 3 days defines the month.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Each family alone "solving its own problems" | Common inventory and roles by day 1 |
| Return to damaged homes for things | Nothing is worth an aftershock on top of you |
| Leave sanitation "for later" | Latrines by day 1-2: the epidemic does not wait |
| Use all the bottled water first | Treat sources starting NOW and reserve the bottled water |
| Rumors and shouted decisions | Information board + fixed short meetings |
| Hoard in silence | What is written and distributed lasts longer and prevents conflict |

## The 72 h community kit

- List of people by household (and who is missing) — the mesh shares it.
- Water: source + treatment + distribution with a measured amount.
- Health: first-aid kits together + whoever knows how to use them + app guides.
- Energy: one generator for ALL phones > five lit houses.

## Practical example: The First 72 Hours

**Situation:** Strong earthquake at dawn; your block has 3 damaged houses, one mildly injured person, and no authority arrives.

**Do:** 1. House-by-house count and injured person treated with the guides. 2. Block gas shut off, wires and cracks marked on the mesh map. 3. 15 min meeting: inventory, common water, and trench latrine at the back of the large lot. 4. Shifts: water, children/older adults, night watch. 5. Board on the corner: who is OK, what is missing, next meeting.

**Avoid:** Do not enter houses with cracks, do not burn trash next to the water, do not leave older adults out of the census, do not wait "for someone to arrive" to organize.

**Escalate:** Severe injuries or someone trapped: triage/collapse guides + mesh SOS to neighboring blocks — and if diarrhea appears in several houses on day 2-3, the water is failing: change the source and reinforce latrines and chlorine NOW.
