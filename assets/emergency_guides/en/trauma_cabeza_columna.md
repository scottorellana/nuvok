# Head and spine trauma

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care. Get medical help as soon as possible.

## When to suspect a SPINAL injury

After: a fall from higher than their own height, a car/motorcycle crash, diving into shallow water, a heavy blow to head or back, or if the person:
- Has neck or back pain.
- Feels tingling, weakness, or can't move arms/legs.
- Is unconscious after a hard impact (assume spine injury until proven otherwise).

## What NOT to do

- ❌ DON'T move them or bend their neck. DON'T sit them up. DON'T rush them into a car without immobilizing.
- ❌ DON'T remove a motorcyclist's helmet unless they're not breathing and you can't manage the airway with it on (then two people remove it, one holding the neck).
- ❌ NO food, drink or pills after a head injury.
- ❌ DON'T plug clear fluid or blood leaking from ear/nose: cover loosely to absorb.
- ❌ DON'T leave them to sleep UNWATCHED the first hours (sleep is allowed, but with checks).

## Steps — suspected spine

1. **Tell them NOT to move.** Kneel behind their head and **hold it steady with both hands** on the sides (ears between your fingers), in the position found, aligned with the body. Stay there.
2. Send someone for help. You do not let go of the head.
3. Alone for a long time? Improvise: sand-filled bags/shoes or rolled towels on both sides of the head, forehead strapped gently to a rigid surface.
4. **If they vomit:** roll them AS ONE UNIT (head-shoulders-hips together, ideally 2-3 people, you commanding the head: "on three").
5. **If breathing stops:** CPR overrides the spine — open the airway by pushing the jaw upward without extending the neck, and compress.
6. Transport ONLY on a rigid surface (board, door), moved as a unit by several people.

## Steps — head knock (conscious, no spine signs)

1. Sit or lay them with head and shoulders slightly raised.
2. Cloth-wrapped cold on the bump, 15-20 min.
3. **Watch for 24-48 hours** (wake them every 2-3 h the first night): answers well, equal pupils, no vomiting.

## When to get help / transport — URGENT if any of these appears:

- Lost consciousness (even if "fine now").
- Repeated vomiting, WORSENING headache, seizure.
- Confusion, odd speech, doesn't recognize people, very drowsy (can't stay awake).
- One pupil bigger than the other; double vision.
- Clear fluid or blood from nose/ear; bruises around both eyes or behind the ears.
- Weakness or tingling anywhere.
- A baby/small child hit hard, or someone on blood thinners.

## Signs of deterioration

- More and more sleepy, slower answers → the brain is being compressed: transport immediately, don't wait "to see if it improves".

## Example: Head injury and spinal injury

**Situation:** After a bicycle crash, the person is awake but reports neck pain and dizziness.

**Do:** 1. Make the scene safe and assign one specific person to call emergency services. 2. Follow the large steps in this guide in order instead of jumping to the most dramatic action. 3. Use gloves, barriers, or clean cloth if available. 4. Recheck every minute: breathing, responsiveness, bleeding, temperature, or worsening signs.

**Avoid:** Do not improvise advanced procedures, do not move the neck/spine if injury is suspected, do not give food or drink to a confused person, and do not delay calling because you are searching for perfect equipment.

**Escalate:** Call or evacuate immediately if breathing is not normal, chest pain appears, bleeding does not stop, confusion worsens, a burn is large, poisoning is possible, pregnancy is advanced, or there are multiple victims.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who is responsible next. This is a guided practice example; adapt it to your environment and prioritize professional help when available.
