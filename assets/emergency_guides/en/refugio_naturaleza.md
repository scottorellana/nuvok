# Shelter in Nature

**Rule of 3:** you can die in **3 hours** from exposure to cold/wind/rain. Shelter comes BEFORE water and food.

## Critical steps

1. **Decide early.** Start the shelter at least 2 hours before sunset. In the dark, everything takes three times as long.
2. **Choose the right place.** Flat, dry ground, protected from the wind, near materials (branches, leaves). Check ABOVE: no dead branches hanging ("widowmakers").
3. **Insulate the ground first.** The ground steals more heat than the air. A layer of 20-30 cm of pine branches, dry leaves, or grass BEFORE thinking about a roof.
4. **Build small.** The ideal shelter is barely larger than your body: your heat warms it. A large shelter is a cold shelter.
5. **Build a lean-to.** A strong log or branch supported between two trees at hip height; branches leaned against it on the windward side; weave thin branches over it.
6. **Roof in layers like shingles.** Leaves, bark, grass, or pine branches from the BOTTOM upward, each layer covering the previous one — this way rain runs off to the outside. Minimum thickness: 30 cm (you must not see light through it).
7. **Close the windward side and mark your shelter** with something visible (colored clothing, a cross made of branches) so rescuers can see it.

## Where NOT to build the shelter

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Bottom of a ravine or gully — cold air sinks and stagnates, and distant rain can flood it | Low slope, halfway up |
| Under dead branches or dry trees | Check the crown BEFORE building |
| Top of a hill exposed to the wind | Sheltered by rocks or dense vegetation |
| Dry streambeds, however comfortable they may seem | At least 60 m from streambeds and banks |
| Next to nests, burrows, or animal trails | Area with no tracks or droppings |

## Types depending on what you have

### With tarp or plastic
- Rope between two trees + tarp over it in an A-frame = shelter in 10 minutes.
- Stake the edges with rocks or branches.

### Debris hut (no tools, cold weather)
- Frame: a long beam supported on a fork (dinosaur-rib shape).
- Side ribs every 30 cm + fill with 60-90 cm of dry leaves ON TOP and INSIDE.
- You crawl in and cover the entrance with a backpack or leaves: it is your natural sleeping bag.

### Snow
- NEVER sleep directly on snow: use a thick layer of pine branches.
- A trench with a roof of branches and snow on top insulates against the wind.

## Keeping heat

- Dry clothing ALWAYS: wet clothing kills. Change or wring it out before sleeping.
- Stones heated next to the fire (NEVER red-hot), wrapped in cloth, inside the shelter.
- Sleep in the fetal position on the insulation if the cold is severe.

## Example: Shelter in Nature

**Situation:** Night is falling in the mountains, it is cold, and you will not reach the town.

**Do:** 1. Stop NOW: there are 2 hours of daylight left; dedicate them to the shelter. 2. A dry low slope, with no dead branches above. 3. Gather leaves and branches: 30 cm of bedding on the ground first. 4. Small lean-to against the wind, roof of 30 cm in layers.

**Avoid:** Do not keep walking in the dark, do not sleep on bare ground, do not build in dry streambeds or under dead branches, do not make a large shelter.

**Escalate:** If someone shows hypothermia (uncontrollable shivering, confusion), prioritize shared body heat and fire; activate the mesh SOS and wait until dawn to move.
