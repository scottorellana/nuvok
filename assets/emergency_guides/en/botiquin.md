# Recommended first-aid kit

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care.

A kit doesn't save lives — **hands that know how to use it do.** Build yours from this list and review it every 6 months (expiry dates, used items).

## The core that actually gets used (home AND backpack)

**Bleeding (most important):**
- Large sterile gauze pads (10×10 cm / 4×4 in) — lots; it's what runs out
- Elastic bandages, 10 cm and 5 cm
- Triangular bandage ×2 (sling, ties, packing)
- Commercial tourniquet (or know how to improvise: guide "Severe bleeding")
- Nitrile gloves, 4+ pairs

**Wounds:**
- Small neutral soap and/or saline ampoules (washing IS the treatment)
- Antiseptic (chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine)
- Assorted adhesive bandages, medical tape
- Wound-closure strips (steri-strips) for gaping cuts
- Fine-point tweezers (splinters, stingers, ticks), trauma shears (cut clothes)

**Over-the-counter medicines (respect labels and each person's allergies):**
- Pain/fever: paracetamol and ibuprofen
- **Aspirin** (besides pain: chewed for suspected heart attack — guide "Heart attack")
- Antihistamine (allergies, stings)
- **Oral rehydration salts ×4 sachets** (diarrhea/dehydration: saves more lives than almost anything)
- Antacid; anti-diarrheal for adults
- Burn gel / aloe; simple antibiotic ointment

**Tools:**
- Thermometer
- Emergency thermal blanket ×2 (hypothermia and shock — weighs nothing)
- Headlamp + spare batteries
- Zip-lock plastic bags (improvised ice pack, improvised gloves, sealing, storage)
- Kitchen plastic film (burns, seals)
- Small notebook + pen (times, tourniquets, victim details)
- Whistle

## Extras for the VEHICLE

- Reflective vest and triangles
- Small ABC fire extinguisher
- Bottled water (2 L minimum)
- Regular blanket + the thermal one
- Work gloves (glass, metal)

## Extras for the 72-hour BACKPACK

- Water (3 L/person/day) + purification tablets or filter
- Long-life food (bars, pull-tab cans)
- Small battery radio; batteries
- Paper copies of documents and phone numbers
- Personal medicines for 7 days (whatever each person takes daily!)
- Charger/power bank for the phone and this Prepper Pad device

## Personal medicines (what nobody can lend you)

- If anyone in the family uses: an inhaler, insulin, an epinephrine auto-injector (severe allergies), anticonvulsants, heart medicines → **keep a duplicate in the kit** and everyone must know where it is and how it's used.

## Maintenance

- Every 6 months: expiry dates, batteries, whatever got used.
- Everyone at home should know WHERE it is and have read this app's guides (Emergency → CPR, Bleeding, Choking are the three vital ones).

## Example practice: Recommended first-aid kit (home, vehicle, backpack)

**Situation:** You are checking the family first-aid kit before storm season and find expired items.

**Do:** 1. Pause for 30 seconds and name the main danger. 2. Gather water, warmth, light, communication, and a safe route. 3. Take the simplest action that reduces risk right now. 4. Record what you did in notes/checklists so another person can continue.

**Avoid:** Do not drain battery without a plan, do not cross moving water, do not eat unknown plants, do not split the group, and do not wait until dark to build shelter.

**Escalate:** Change plans if weather worsens, body temperature drops, safe water is running out, vulnerable people are present, or you lose orientation.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who is responsible next. This is a guided practice example; adapt it to your environment and prioritize professional help when available.
