# River Floods

The river rises **faster than you can run carrying camp**. A flood is won BEFORE it comes: where you set up, what you watch, and when you leave.

## Critical steps

1. **Camp HIGH: the 5-meter rule.** Never on the inner beach of the bend, on islands, or on sandbars — they are the riverbed on loan. Find the high terrace, look for marks of past floods (stranded logs, combed-down grass) and stay ABOVE them.
2. **The sky UPSTREAM rules.** Thunder or rain in the mountains even if you have sun: the river can rise in minutes. Cloudy upper basin = camp on alert.
3. **Plant your witness stick.** A branch driven in at the water's edge when you arrive: if the water covers it or the river sounds deeper, BREAK camp — do not argue with the trend.
4. **Signs a flood is ALREADY underway:** the water turns murky all at once, carries twigs and foam, the roar grows, it smells of churned earth. Any one of these = everyone to the high terrace, NOW.
5. **Evacuate UPHILL, not downstream.** The flood travels with you if you flee along the bank. Climb perpendicular to the river to ground that overlooks the valley.
6. **Trapped on an island or in a car: to the roof/high point and SOS.** Do not try to wade a flood (30 cm of fast water knocks down an adult; 60 cm sweeps away a car). Wait: flash floods drop within hours.
7. **After the flood, distrust the ground.** Undercut banks that collapse, new holes, unstable logs: walk far from the edge for 24-48 h.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Camping on the little beach "right by the water, so comfortable" | High terrace, above the old flood marks |
| Trusting the blue sky overhead | Watch the basin UPSTREAM; the river comes from far away |
| Staying to pack up the WHOLE camp | People first, things later: the river does not wait |
| Wading the flood "it barely reaches the knee" | 30 cm of fast water knocks you down; wait for it to drop |
| Fleeing along the bank downstream | Climb perpendicular to high ground |
| Approaching the undercut edge to look | Banks collapse after a flood |

## Rainy-season crossings and camp

- Cross early in the morning: mountain rivers rise in the afternoon with the daily melt/rain.
- ALWAYS locate your vertical escape route before sleeping: in 60 seconds, which way do you climb?
- Children and non-swimmers sleep on the HIGH side of camp.

## Example: River Flood

**Situation:** Camp 3 m from the river; at nightfall it thunders hard up the mountain although you have stars overhead.

**Do:** 1. Camp alert: shoes on, flashlights at hand. 2. Check your witness stick every 15 min. 3. The water covered the stick and foam is arriving: EVERYONE climbs NOW with what they are wearing to the high terrace. 4. Head count up top; nobody goes back for things. 5. Trigger the mesh SOS if anyone is cut off.

**Avoid:** Do not sleep "one more hour to see if it passes", do not cross to the "better" far bank, do not go back for the backpack, do not go down until the water clears and drops steadily.

**Escalate:** If someone is swept away: do NOT jump in — run along the bank to get ahead of them, throw a rope/long branch at a slow stretch and shout instructions (feet downstream, river crossing guide); mark the last point where you saw them for the rescuers.
