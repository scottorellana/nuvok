# Community Drinking Water

A neighborhood holds together if **safe water is a system, not a matter of luck**: source + treatment + fair distribution, operated by neighbors.

## Critical steps

1. **Choose ONE main source** and protect it: well, river at its best stretch, or harvesting from large roofs (school/church). Single source = concentrated treatment and monitoring.
2. **Physically protect the source:** a perimeter where laundry is NOT washed, animals do not drink, and the latrine does not come near (minimum 30 m and the latrine downhill). A sign and a rope are already “infrastructure.”
3. **Simple treatment station:** settling in numbered drums (day 1, day 2), coarse filtration (cloth/sand), and measured chlorination with the app’s calculator (2 drops/L at 5% — at scale: 1 teaspoon ≈ 100 drops ≈ 50 L clear containers).
4. **Home residual chlorine test:** at 30 min the water should smell VERY slightly of chlorine; no smell = repeat the dose once; strong smell = too much dose, ventilate and adjust the next batch.
5. **Measured distribution and schedule:** X liters per person/day (start at 5-7 L), fixed turns, and designated carriers for older/sick people. A line with rules prevents the fight that breaks everything.
6. **Water logbook:** how much was treated, with how much chlorine, who operated. If someone gets sick, it can be traced; the knowledge remains when the operator rests.
7. **Watch for the failure signal:** diarrhea in several homes = review the WHOLE system today (contaminated source, dirty drum, expired chlorine) and switch to boiling in the meantime.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Each family treating water “their own way” | Single station with measured dose and logbook |
| Latrines or washing areas beside the source | 30+ m and downhill |
| Chlorinating turbid water directly | ALWAYS settle and filter before chlorine |
| Distribution “first come, first served” | Measure per person, schedule, and carriers |
| Uncovered and unnumbered drums | Covered, rotated, and with day marked |
| Hiding cases of diarrhea | Report NOW: they are the system alarm |

## Scale the system

- 50 houses ≈ 1,500-2,000 L/day minimum: 10 drums of 200 L in rotation.
- Two operators per shift, relief every 3 days (fatigue leads to bad dosing).
- Rain: every public roof to drums — the system grows for free.

## Practical example: Community Water

**Situation:** Week 1 after the hurricane; 40 families, the school well works but people form a chaotic line with dirty containers.

**Do:** 1. Short meeting: the well will be THE source, with perimeter and schedule. 2. Station: 6 numbered drums — settle, filter, chlorinate with the calculator, and wait 30 min. 3. Distribution: 7 L/person/day, line with list, carriers for 8 homes of older people. 4. Logbook attached to the drum + smell test each batch. 5. Containers: covered only; jerrycan washing station separate.

**Avoid:** Do not let laundry be washed at the well, do not chlorinate without settling, do not distribute without measuring, do not use chemical jerrycans.

**Escalate:** Diarrhea in 3+ homes: immediate general boiling, review of source/drums/dose, oral rehydration solution with the calculator for those affected, and notice over the mesh to the whole neighborhood until the failure is found.
