# Latrines and Sanitation

After the disaster, **sanitation decides whether week 2 brings an epidemic**: cholera does not arrive by bad luck but because of feces near water and unwashed hands.

## Critical steps

1. **Trench latrine on DAY 1-2:** trench 30 cm wide × 90 cm deep × as long as possible, 30+ m from all water and DOWNHILL from wells/source. Sturdy board or logs to support the feet, and soil/ash beside it.
2. **The one-cup-of-soil rule:** every use is covered with soil or ash — stops odor, flies, and the chain of transmission. A covered bucket for used paper if the trench is narrow.
3. **Handwashing station REQUIRED at the exit:** tippy-tap (hanging jug that tips with the foot) + tied-on soap. Without a handwashing station, the latrine is only a place to gather and get sick.
4. **Privacy and safety:** tarp/sack walls, path light at night, and women’s/children’s latrines closer to the settlement — an unsafe latrine at night makes people go back to the woods.
5. **Life cycle:** at 30 cm from the top edge, close it by covering with 40 cm of compacted soil, mark it, and open the next trench. Never reopen one that has been closed.
6. **Trash in its own pit:** organic matter to compost/pit away from water; what burns, safely burned downwind away from houses; cans/glass crushed in a separate pit. Scattered trash brings rats in days.
7. **Graywater with an outlet:** wash water must drain to a gravel trench/soak pit, not pool — the puddle breeds mosquitoes (dengue) in 72 h.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| “Everyone goes to the woods for now” | Common trench on day 1: the woods contaminate everyone’s water |
| Latrine uphill from the well | 30+ m and ALWAYS downhill |
| No soil/ash beside the trench | Cover every use: flies = epidemic |
| Latrine without a handwashing station with soap | Tippy-tap required at the exit |
| Fill the trench to the top | Close at 30 cm from the edge and open another |
| Puddles of wash water everywhere | Drainage to gravel: dengue breeds there |

## Hygiene that sustains the neighborhood

- Hands with soap: before eating/cooking, after the latrine, and after touching the injured.
- Water+soap point also in the communal kitchen and where the injured are treated.
- Children: “tippy-tap inspectors” shift — keeps the system alive and educates them.

## Practical example: Latrines and Sanitation

**Situation:** Camp of 25 families after the flood; by day 2 people are going to the brush by the river and there are already flies in the communal kitchen.

**Do:** 1. TODAY: two trenches (one family, one for women/children) 40 m from the river, downhill from the camp. 2. Soil+ash with a shovel at each one, sack walls. 3. Two tippy-taps with soap tied on at the exit. 4. Trash pit + controlled burning downwind. 5. Announcement at the meeting: the brush is prohibited and WHY is explained (the river is everyone’s water).

**Avoid:** Do not put the latrine “nearby for convenience,” do not leave uses uncovered, do not dump wash water onto the path, do not burn trash next to the tarps.

**Scale up:** First case of severe diarrhea or vomiting: isolate their feces (separate latrine if possible), oral rehydration solution (calculator), reinforced chlorine in the water, and notice over the mesh; 3+ cases = health emergency — everything boiled, hands monitored, and seek medical evacuation.
