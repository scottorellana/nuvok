# Water in the Desert

In the desert, water IS life: without it you have **1-2 days in extreme heat**. Every decision is measured in sweat.

## Critical steps

1. **Drink the water you have.** The classic fatal mistake is "saving it": people die dehydrated with water in their canteen. Your body stores it better than your bottle.
2. **Ration SWEAT, not water.** Move only at dawn, dusk or night; rest in shade the rest of the day. Walking at midday costs 1 liter per hour.
3. **Search the dry washes.** Dig 30-60 cm at the OUTSIDE bend of a dry stream bed, where sand looks darker or plants grow. If the sand turns damp, wait for water to seep into the hole.
4. **Follow the signs of life.** Birds at dawn/dusk fly TOWARD water; insect swarms, converging animal tracks and green patches of vegetation mean water is near.
5. **Harvest dawn dew.** Before sunrise, drag an absorbent cloth through vegetation and wring it out; on cold nights smooth rocks and metal surfaces also condense water.
6. **Check the rock tanks.** Shaded hollows and crevices in rock formations (tinajas) hold rainwater for weeks after a storm.
7. **Purify WHENEVER you can.** Water from tanks or dug holes: boil it or use your kit's chlorine drops (2 drops/liter at 5%, 30 min). Diarrhea in the desert is a death sentence.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|-----------|-----------|
| "Saving" water while walking thirsty | Drink when thirsty; ration effort instead |
| Drinking cactus juice — the myth that kills | Most cause vomiting and diarrhea: you lose MORE water |
| Walking at midday looking for water | Search at dawn/dusk; shade at midday |
| Drinking urine or radiator water | They speed up dehydration and poison you |
| Eating with less than 1 liter in reserve | Digestion uses water: no water, no food |
| Betting everything on a solar still | It yields little (a cup/day); it's support, not salvation |

## Solar still (support, not a miracle)

1. Dig a hole 60 cm wide × 40 cm deep in moist ground (dry wash) or add crushed green vegetation.
2. Center a clean container at the bottom.
3. Cover the hole with clear plastic sealed with rocks/sand at the edges.
4. Place a small stone at the center of the plastic, right over the container: the cone drips condensation inside.
5. Wait 6-12 h. Yields ~1 cup a day: build several if you depend on them.

## Signs of dangerous dehydration

- Dark urine or no urine for 8+ hours.
- Dizziness on standing, pounding headache.
- Skin that doesn't spring back when pinched, pasty mouth with no saliva.
- Confusion or irritability → switch to the heat stroke guide: it's an emergency NOW.

## Example: Water in the Desert

**Situation:** Your vehicle broke down on a desert road; 1 liter per person remains and rescue may take more than a day.

**Do:** 1. Stay with the vehicle (shade + visibility). 2. Drink with your thirst, don't "save". 3. At first and last light search nearby dry washes and dig at the outside bend. 4. Build one solar still per person with whatever plastic you have. 5. Mark your position and trigger the mesh SOS.

**Avoid:** Don't walk at midday, don't drink from cactus or urine, don't eat if water is short, don't leave the vehicle without marking your heading.

**Escalate:** If someone stops sweating, becomes confused or collapses, it's heat stroke: shade NOW, wet their clothes, fan them and switch to that guide — it is the desert's number one emergency.
