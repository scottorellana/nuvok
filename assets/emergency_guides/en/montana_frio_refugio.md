# Cold and Shelter in the Mountains

In the mountains the enemy is the triad **wind + moisture + altitude**: wind steals heat 10 times faster, and wet clothing is a death sentence.

## Critical steps

1. **Dry > warm.** Wet clothing (rain OR sweat) loses 90% of its insulation. Adjust layers BEFORE you sweat on the climb; change out of wet clothes BEFORE stopping.
2. **Block the WIND first.** At -5 °C with strong wind the effect is -20 °C. Any barrier (rock, hollow on the sheltered side, snow wall) is worth more than a campfire in the wind.
3. **Get off the summit, avoid the valley floor.** Shelter goes at MID-slope on the protected side: the summit takes the wind and the valley floor collects the freezing night air.
4. **Insulate the ground twice as much as the roof.** 20-30 cm of pine branches/dry grass: frozen ground steals more heat than the air.
5. **In deep snow: trench or cave.** A trench your size + roof of branches and snow, or a cave in a slope of compact snow (domed ceiling, air hole kept open with a stick, sleeping platform HIGH — cold sinks).
6. **Small fire with a reflector.** Against a rock or wall that bounces heat toward the shelter; heated stones wrapped in cloth inside your clothing.
7. **Watch the group for hypothermia.** Uncontrollable shivering, clumsiness, slurred speech, "I'm not cold anymore": emergency — hypothermia guide NOW, shared body heat.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Climbing on while pouring sweat | Adjust layers: cold at the start, mild on the move |
| Sheltering on the summit or the valley floor | Mid-slope, on the side protected from the wind |
| Sleeping directly on snow/frozen ground | 20-30 cm of plant insulation ALWAYS |
| Sealing the snow cave airtight | Air hole always open (CO₂ kills in your sleep) |
| Drinking alcohol "for the cold" | It dilates vessels: warms for 5 min and chills you the rest |
| Waiting until night to decide on shelter | 2 hours of light minimum to build |

## The layering rule

- **Base:** synthetic or wool (NEVER cotton: wet, it chills you).
- **Middle:** insulation (fleece/down) — comes off when working hard.
- **Outer:** wind/rain shell ALWAYS at hand.
- Hat and gloves: head and hands lose a disproportionate amount of heat.

## Example: Cold in the Mountains

**Situation:** Fog closed off the descent in mid-afternoon; you are sweaty, the wind is blowing and 2 hours of daylight remain.

**Do:** 1. STOP: decide on shelter now, not "a little farther down." 2. Protected mid-slope: a hollow behind a large rock. 3. Change out of the wet layer; put on EVERYTHING dry. 4. 30 cm of branches on the ground, snow/branch wall against the wind. 5. Small fire against the rock + hot stones wrapped in cloth; trigger the mesh SOS.

**Avoid:** Do not keep descending in the dark with fog, do not sleep in wet clothes, do not build on the summit or the valley floor, do not waste the daylight hesitating.

**Escalate:** If someone stops shivering and grows drowsy in the cold, it is severe hypothermia: hypothermia guide, skin-to-skin body heat, no rubbing and no alcohol; at dawn consider descending only if the whole group can.
