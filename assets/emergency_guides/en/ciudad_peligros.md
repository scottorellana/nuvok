# Hazards of the Collapsed City

The broken city kills silently: **the gas you stop smelling after a while, the cable in the puddle, the wall that held "by a miracle"** — and people's panic.

## Critical steps

1. **Gas: nose first, valve after.** Rotten-egg smell or hissing = do NOT switch on ANYTHING (not even light switches: they spark). Ventilate by opening up, shut the main gas valve, evacuate and warn the neighbors by knocking on doors — no doorbell.
2. **Every downed cable is LIVE** until the power company says otherwise. Keep a bus length away (10 m); in puddles, water extends the reach. If a cable falls on your car: stay inside and wait for help.
3. **Damaged structures are re-assessed after every aftershock.** X-shaped cracks in columns, uneven floors, doors that no longer close = NOBODY sleeps inside. Better uncomfortable in the yard than fast asleep under the slab.
4. **Fire with no firefighters: win the exit, not the battle.** Smoke = crawl (breathable air is low), door hot to the back of the hand = do NOT open it, wet cloth over the mouth. Put out only wastebasket-size fires; everything else: evacuate and contain (close doors as you leave).
5. **Move by day, in a group and with a set course.** After the collapse the streets change: avoid passing under damaged structures, leaning poles and panicking crowds. Always two alternate routes.
6. **Security without heroics:** low profile, resources stored out of sight, doors reinforced simply (wedge, furniture), and the neighbor network as the best alarm — the neighborhood that knows itself protects itself (community organization guide).
7. **Document and communicate:** photo of your house's damage (for later) and the state of routes/hazards shared over the mesh — your "bridge down" notice saves whoever comes behind (tactical map).

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Turning on the light "to check the gas leak" | Nothing electrical: ventilate, shut the valve, get out |
| Stepping over the "dead" downed cable | 10 m of distance; live until confirmed |
| Sleeping inside "because it survived the quake" | X-shaped cracks or jammed doors = outside |
| Opening the hot door during the fire | Back of the hand first; hot = another exit |
| Showing off generator/pantry/weapon | Low profile and neighbor network |
| Crossing the panicking crowd against the flow | Edges of the mass, never against the center |

## The urban hazards kit

- Gas and water valves: EVERYONE at home knows where they are and how to shut them.
- ABC extinguisher or bucket of sand in the kitchen.
- Closed shoes NEXT to the bed (glass) + flashlight + whistle.
- Copies of documents in a sealed bag + the app's ICE card.

## Example: Urban Hazards

**Situation:** After the quake your block has no power; you smell gas outside the neighbor's house and there is a cable on the wet sidewalk.

**Do:** 1. Mark a mental 10 m around the cable and warn people by shouting from afar — nobody crosses the puddle. 2. At the house with gas: knock on the door (no doorbell), have them shut the valve and ventilate without touching switches. 3. Shut off YOUR house's gas too, just in case. 4. Post both hazards on the mesh tactical map. 5. Tonight: check for cracks before sleeping inside.

**Avoid:** Do not light your lighter to "see better", do not move the cable with a "dry" stick, do not let children wander nearby, do not sleep in the cracked house.

**Escalate:** Explosion or open fire: evacuate the block upwind, mesh meeting point active, and the burns/collapse guides take over; electrical casualties = CPR if unresponsive (they are safe to touch once separated from the source).
