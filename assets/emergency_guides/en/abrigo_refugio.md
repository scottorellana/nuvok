# Shelter and Warmth in Emergencies

## 3-3-3 Rule

- 3 hours without warmth in cold = hypothermia
- 3 days without water = death
- 3 weeks without food = death

## Types of Shelter

### Urgent (immediate)
- **Enclosed vehicle** — better than nothing
- **Ditch** — protects from wind
- **Stone/snow wall** — breaks wind

### Improvised
- **Branch lean-to** — long side facing wind
- **Snow shelter** — if enough snow
- **Tarp with plastic** — any plastic works

## Basic Construction

### Emergency shelter
1. Find natural slope or dig hole
2. Cover with branches, then dirt/leaves
3. Entrance opposite to wind
4. Elevated bed (insulation)

### Quick tarp
```
    /\
   /  \  <- plastic/poncho
  /____\
```

## Body Temperature

### Hypothermia
**Signs:**
- Uncontrollable shivering
- Confusion, slurred speech
- Loss of coordination
- Extreme drowsiness

**Treatment:**
1. Remove wet clothes
2. Skin-to-skin if possible
3. Warm drink if conscious
4. Don't rub extremities

### Heat Stroke
**Signs:**
- Hot and dry skin
- Confusion
- Rapid, strong pulse
- Temperature >40°C

**Treatment:**
1. Move to shade
2. Cool with water
3. Don't give fluids if unconscious
4. Seek medical help

## Clothing

### Layer system
1. **Base** — moisture away from body
2. **Insulation** — retains heat
3. **Protection** — wind/rain

### Priorities
- Cover head (50% body heat)
- Hands and feet
- Neck

## Fire

### Quick methods
- Flint + metal
- Sunglasses / magnifying glass
- Battery + aluminum foil
- Friction (bow drill)

### Dry materials
- Dry tree bark
- Paracord
- Small dry sticks
- Bones with marrow

## Example practice: Shelter and Warmth

**Situation:** A storm catches the group near sunset and clothing is starting to get wet.

**Do:** 1. Pause for 30 seconds and name the main danger. 2. Gather water, warmth, light, communication, and a safe route. 3. Take the simplest action that reduces risk right now. 4. Record what you did in notes/checklists so another person can continue.

**Avoid:** Do not drain battery without a plan, do not cross moving water, do not eat unknown plants, do not split the group, and do not wait until dark to build shelter.

**Escalate:** Change plans if weather worsens, body temperature drops, safe water is running out, vulnerable people are present, or you lose orientation.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who is responsible next. This is a guided practice example; adapt it to your environment and prioritize professional help when available.
