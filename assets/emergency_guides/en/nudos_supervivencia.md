# Knots that Save Lives

Five knots cover 95% of survival: rescuing, tying off, tensioning, joining and building. **Practice them TODAY with a shoelace** — in an emergency your hands shake and there is no time to learn.

## Critical steps

1. **Bowline — the rescue knot.** A FIXED loop that will not tighten around what it holds: for hoisting or securing a person. "The rabbit comes out of the hole, around the tree, back into the hole." Unties easily even after heavy load.
2. **Clove hitch — fast tie to a pole.** Two crossed turns around a log/post. Starts and ends structures (shelter, raft). CAUTION: it loosens under on-off jerks — finish with a half hitch.
3. **Taut-line hitch — the adjustable line.** A sliding knot that GRIPS under tension: tarp lines, shelter guy-lines, clotheslines. Slides by hand, locks under load.
4. **Double fisherman's — joins two ropes.** The safe way to extend rope, even different thicknesses and wet. Two knots that hug each other; compact and does not slip.
5. **Square lashing — joins poles in a cross.** For structures: shelter, raft, stretcher, tripod. Clove hitch to start + 3 wraps each axis + 2 frapping turns pulled tight + clove hitch to finish.

## Which knot when

| I need to… | Knot |
|------------|------|
| Raise/lower/secure a PERSON | Bowline |
| Start or finish a lashing on a pole | Clove hitch + half hitch |
| Tension a tarp or guy-line | Taut-line hitch |
| Extend rope with another rope | Double fisherman's |
| Join two poles firmly (stretcher, raft, shelter) | Square lashing |

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|-----------|-----------|
| Hoisting a person with a slip knot | Bowline: the loop must NOT close on the chest |
| Trusting a knot you can't retie blindfolded | Practice 20 times before you need it |
| Joining ropes with a simple overhand | Double fisherman's; the overhand slips under load |
| Leaving tails shorter than 10 cm | Leave generous tails: knots "eat" rope under load |
| Using damaged rope for human loads | Inspect the whole rope; one cut strand = never for rescue |

## Rope care

- Sand and sun kill rope: store it coiled, shaded and clean.
- Wet rope loses ~10-15% strength; knot with margin.
- For human loads plan a 10× margin: 80 kg person → rope rated 800 kg minimum.

## Example: Knots that Save Lives

**Situation:** A companion fell into a 3-meter gully and you have good rope.

**Do:** 1. Bowline: a fixed loop that will NOT tighten on their chest. 2. Pass it under their armpits. 3. Anchor your end to a tree with a clove hitch + half hitch. 4. Haul with the rope over your shoulder, walking — not with bare arms.

**Avoid:** Don't use a slip knot on a person, don't improvise a knot you haven't mastered, don't haul on damaged rope, don't leave tails under 10 cm.

**Escalate:** If the person cannot hold on or fell with head/spine impact, do not move them with rope: stabilize them in place (trauma guide) and call for help on the emergency channel.
