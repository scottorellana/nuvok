# Fire Without Matches

Fire gives warmth, purifies water, cooks, lights the night and **signals rescuers**. Preparing it well matters more than lighting it.

## Critical steps

1. **Prepare EVERYTHING before the first spark.** Tinder + thin kindling + medium wood + thick wood, all dry and within arm's reach. 90% of failed fires die from lack of preparation, not lack of spark.
2. **Collect real tinder.** Dry and fluffy: inner bark, shredded dry grass, cotton from clothing, pocket lint, ultra-fine knife shavings. A BIG handful (grapefruit-sized).
3. **Protect the fire site.** Dry base (bark or flat stones), a wall against the wind, natural roof if drizzling — but with smoke free to rise.
4. **Make the spark/ember** (methods below) directly onto the tinder.
5. **Blow gently and steadily.** When the tinder smokes, lift it in your hands like a nest and blow softly from below — the classic mistake is blowing hard and killing it.
6. **Feed from thin to thick.** Sticks like a match → like a pencil → like a finger → like a wrist. Each size must burn well before the next.
7. **Never leave it unattended** and keep water/dirt ready to kill it completely (buried embers start wildfires).

## Methods without matches

### Ferro rod (the one in your kit)
- Scrape SLOW and HARD with the knife spine, almost touching the tinder.
- 3000 °C sparks: works wet and in wind.

### Lens (strong sun only)
- Magnifier, binocular lens, bottle bottom with clear water.
- Focus the light point as small as possible on DARK tinder and hold still.

### Battery + steel wool or foil
- Steel wool touching both terminals of a 9V battery = instant ember.
- Gum wrapper (foil+paper) cut to a thin waist across an AA battery's terminals.

### Friction (bow drill — the last resort)
- Only with VERY dry, soft wood (willow, cedar).
- Demands technique and hours of practice: don't count on it untrained.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|-----------|-----------|
| Lighting before all the wood is ready | Stage 3 sizes of wood BEFORE the spark |
| "More or less dry" tinder | Hunt for truly dry: inside logs, under rocks |
| Piling thick wood on a newborn flame | Thin to thick, with air between pieces |
| Fire under hanging branches or on dry roots | Clear 1.5 m around and above |
| Boosting with fuel/alcohol | Never: burns and uncontrollable flare |
| Walking away from "almost dead" embers | Water + stir + dirt, cold to the touch |

## Fire as a rescue signal

- **3 fires in a triangle** = international distress signal.
- By DAY: smoke — add green leaves or rubber to a hot fire.
- By NIGHT: flame — keep it big and alive in a visible clearing.

## Example: Fire Without Matches

**Situation:** Dusk, you're wet, and all you carry is a knife and a ferro rod.

**Do:** 1. BEFORE any spark: tinder from inside a dry log + 3 sizes of wood staged. 2. Dry base and a wind wall. 3. Scrape the ferro rod slow and hard over the tinder nest. 4. Blow gently from below and feed thin to thick.

**Avoid:** Don't strike before the wood is staged, don't blow hard on the first ember, don't use fuel, don't leave it unattended.

**Escalate:** If it will not light after many tries and the temperature is dropping, abandon the fire: get into the shelter, insulate the ground and share body heat; retry in daylight.
