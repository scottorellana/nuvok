# Heat and Shelter in the Desert

The desert kills twice: **from heat at midday and from cold in the early morning**. Your shelter and your schedule are your survival.

## Critical steps

1. **Invert your day.** Move ONLY at dawn, at dusk, and (with moonlight) at night. From 10 to 16 h: total shade and stillness. This is the rule that saves the most lives.
2. **Clothing stays ON.** Loose, light-colored, long-sleeved clothing + head and neck covered: fabric blocks the sun and traps sweat to cool you. Taking off your shirt dehydrates you faster and burns the skin.
3. **Separate yourself from the ground.** At midday the ground is 15-20 °C hotter than the air at 30 cm. Lie on a backpack, branches, or stacked stones, never directly on the sand.
4. **DOUBLE roof with an air gap.** Two layers of fabric/tarp separated by 20-30 cm reduce heat by up to 15 °C compared with direct sun: a single layer only gives warm shade.
5. **Use what already exists.** The shade of a north-facing boulder, a rock overhang, or your vehicle (underneath NO: the metal radiates; beside it, on the shaded side) is worth more than building.
6. **Prepare for night BEFORE sunset.** The desert can drop from 35 °C to below 5 °C: gather firewood by day, close the shelter on the windward side, and save a dry layer for sleeping.
7. **Watch for heat stroke in the group.** Dry, hot skin WITHOUT sweating, confusion, staggering gait: immediate shade, wet clothing, fan — and the heat stroke guide.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Walking at midday "to make progress" | Move at dawn/dusk/night |
| Taking off clothing because of the heat | Loose, light-colored clothing ON, head covered |
| Lying directly on hot sand | 30 cm of separation from the ground |
| Taking shelter INSIDE the vehicle in the sun | Shade beside the vehicle, ventilated |
| Ignoring the night ("it’s the desert, it’s hot") | Prepare warm clothing and fire before sunset |
| Waiting for thirst to hydrate during exertion | Small, regular sips while you move |

## Emergency shelter in 20 minutes

1. Look for a natural depression or the shaded side of a large rock.
2. Anchor a tarp/blanket from a high rock to two low stakes (lean-to against the afternoon sun).
3. Second layer (extra shirt, another tarp) 20-30 cm ABOVE the first.
4. Floor: backpacks, branches, or flat stones — never direct sand.
5. Opening toward the breeze; never closed into an oven.

## The desert night

- Real cold: hypothermia is possible after a day of 40 °C.
- Small, steady fire > bonfire; firewood is scarce.
- Stones heated beside the fire, wrapped in cloth, inside the shelter.
- Check boots and clothing at dawn: scorpions seek your warmth (hazards guide).

## Example: Heat and Shelter

**Situation:** Lost hike in arid terrain; it is 11 in the morning, the sun is directly overhead, and there is no town in sight.

**Do:** 1. STOP now: look for the north shade of a boulder. 2. Build a double roof with your tarp and extra shirt, body separated from the ground. 3. Drink in regular sips and stay still until 16-17 h. 4. At dusk: gather firewood, prepare for night, and move only with usable light. 5. Activate the mesh SOS from the shade.

**Avoid:** Do not keep walking at midday, do not take off your shirt, do not get into the car/cave without ventilation, do not leave the night to improvisation.

**Escalate:** If someone stops sweating with hot, dry skin, or becomes confused: it is heat stroke — aggressive cooling NOW (water on clothing + fan) and move to the heat stroke guide; if there are seizures, seizure guide.
