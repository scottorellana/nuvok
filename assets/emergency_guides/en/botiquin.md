# Recommended first-aid kit

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care.

A first-aid kit does not save lives — **the hands that know how to use it do.** Build yours with this list and review it every 6 months (expiration dates, what has been used up).

## The core that actually gets used (home AND backpack)

**Bleeding (the most important):**
- Large sterile gauze pads (10×10) — many, this is what gets used up the most
- Elastic bandages of 10 cm and 5 cm
- Triangular bandage ×2 (sling, ties, packing)
- Commercial tourniquet (or know how to improvise: guide "Severe bleeding")
- Nitrile gloves ×4 pairs minimum

**Wounds:**
- Small mild soap and/or saline solution in ampoules (washing is THE treatment)
- Antiseptic (chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine)
- Bandages of various sizes, medical adhesive tape (micropore)
- Closure strips (Steri-Strips) for cuts that gape open
- Fine-tip tweezers (splinters, stingers, ticks), trauma shears (cut clothing)

**Over-the-counter medicines (respect each person's labels and allergies):**
- Pain reliever/fever reducer: acetaminophen and ibuprofen
- **Aspirin** (in addition to pain: chewed when a heart attack is suspected — guide "Heart attack")
- Antihistamine (allergies, bites/stings)
- **Oral rehydration solution ×4 packets** (diarrhea/dehydration: saves more lives than almost anything)
- Antacid; antidiarrheal for adults
- Cream for minor burns / aloe; simple antibiotic ointment

**Instruments:**
- Thermometer
- Emergency thermal blanket ×2 (hypothermia and shock — weighs nothing)
- Headlamp + spare batteries
- Resealable plastic bags (improvised ice, improvised gloves, seal, store)
- Plastic kitchen wrap (burns, seals)
- Small notebook + pen (times, tourniquets, victim information)
- Whistle

## Extras for the VEHICLE

- Reflective vest and triangles
- Small ABC fire extinguisher
- Bottled water (2 L minimum)
- Regular blanket + the thermal blanket
- Work gloves (glass, sheet metal)

## Extras for the 72-hour BACKPACK

- Water (3 L/person/day) + water purification tablets or filter
- Long-lasting foods (bars, cans with easy-open tops)
- Small battery-powered radio; batteries
- Copy of documents and list of phone numbers ON PAPER
- Personal medicines for 7 days (what each person takes daily!)
- Charger/battery for the phone and this Prepper Pad device

## Personal medicines (what no one can lend you)

- If someone in the family uses: inhaler, insulin, epinephrine auto-injector (severe allergies), anticonvulsants, heart medicines → **duplicate in the first-aid kit** and everyone must know where it is and how it is used.

## Maintenance

- Every 6 months: expiration dates, batteries, what was used up.
- Everyone at home must know WHERE it is and have read the guides in this app (Emergency → CPR, Bleeding, Choking are the three vital ones).

## Example: Recommended first-aid kit (home, vehicle, and backpack)

**Situation:** You are going to check the family first-aid kit before the rainy season and you discover expired items.

**Do:** 1. Stop for 30 seconds and define the main danger. 2. Gather water, warmth, light, communication, and a safe route. 3. Carry out the simplest action that reduces risk now. 4. Mark what was done in notes/checklist so another person can continue.

**Avoid:** Do not use battery without a plan, do not cross moving water, do not eat unknown plants, do not separate from the group, and do not wait until dark to set up shelter.

**Escalate:** Change the plan if the weather worsens, body temperature drops, safe water is lacking, there are vulnerable children/older adults, or you lose orientation.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who remains responsible. This example is guided practice; adapt the steps to your environment and prioritize professional help when it exists.
