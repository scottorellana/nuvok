# CPR in Children and Infants — AHA 2025

**Infant:** younger than 1 year, except a newborn at the time of delivery. **Child:** from 1 year to puberty.

> ⚠️ Activate the local emergency response system. Pediatric CPR with compressions and ventilations is preferable when the rescuer knows how to do it; this guide does not replace hands-on training.

## Recognize cardiac arrest

Act if the child or infant is unresponsive and is not breathing normally or is only gasping. A lay rescuer should not delay CPR to check for a pulse.

## Sequence for one rescuer

1. **Safety and response.** Check the scene. Call to the child and tap their shoulders; for an infant, gently tap the sole of the foot. Do not shake them.
2. **Call for help and an AED.** If you have a phone, call on speaker without leaving the patient. Send someone else to get an AED.
3. **If you are alone and do not have a phone:**
   - for a witnessed sudden collapse, activate emergency services and get the AED first;
   - if the collapse was not witnessed or probably began from lack of oxygen, perform about 2 minutes of CPR before leaving to call and get the AED.
4. **Place them face up on a firm, flat surface.**
5. **Start with compressions.** AHA uses the compressions-airway-breathing sequence; it **does not indicate 5 initial ventilations** in this algorithm.

## High-quality compressions

Perform compressions at **100–120 per minute**, allow complete recoil, and limit each pause to less than 10 seconds.

### Child from 1 year to puberty

- Heel of **one or two hands** in the center of the chest, on the lower half of the sternum.
- Depth: at least one third of the anterior-posterior diameter of the chest, approximately **5 cm**; avoid excessive depth.

### Infant younger than 1 year

- Compress the sternum with **two thumbs side by side while both hands encircle the chest**, or with the **heel of one hand**.
- If you cannot encircle the chest, use the heel of one hand.
- Depth: at least one third of the chest, approximately **4 cm**.
- The old two-finger technique **is no longer recommended for infant CPR in AHA 2025**.

## Compression-to-ventilation ratio

| Rescuers | Ratio |
|---|---|
| One | **30:2** |
| Two trained rescuers | **15:2** |

To ventilate:

- Child: gently tilt the head and lift the chin; pinch the nose and seal the mouth.
- Infant: keep the head in a neutral or gentle sniffing position and seal the mouth and nose with your mouth.
- Give each ventilation for approximately 1 second, only until you see the chest rise. If it does not rise, reposition the airway before trying again.

If you cannot give ventilations, perform compressions without stopping: it is better than doing no CPR.

## AED

- Turn it on and follow its prompts as soon as it is available.
- Use pediatric pads/attenuator if available. If they are not available, adult pads may be used if they fit without touching each other.
- If the pads would touch, place one on the chest and the other on the back.
- Resume compressions immediately after each analysis or shock.

## Continue and monitor

Continue until they breathe normally and show signs of life, trained help arrives, someone relieves you, or you cannot continue. If breathing returns but they do not wake up, place them on their side only if there is no injury that prevents it and monitor breathing without pause.

## Dangerous errors

- Do not shake an infant or rub forcefully over the sternum.
- Do not hyperextend the infant's neck.
- Do not use the obsolete two-finger technique for CPR compressions.
- Do not delay compressions to look for a pulse if you are a lay rescuer.
- Do not give large or fast ventilations; visible chest rise is enough.

## Example: CPR in Children and Babies

**Situation:** A 8-month-old baby becomes limp and unresponsive after a choking episode; they are not breathing normally.

**Do:** 1. Shout for help and ask someone to call emergency services on speaker. 2. Place them on a firm surface. 3. Encircle the chest with both hands and compress in the center with two thumbs side by side, about 4 cm deep, at 100–120 per minute. 4. Alone: 30:2; with two trained rescuers: 15:2. 5. After 2 minutes, if no one has called, call yourself and return immediately.

**Avoid:** Do not shake the baby, do not compress over the abdomen or ribs, do not interrupt for more than 10 seconds, do not give up on a cold or blue baby: continue.

**Escalate:** If the chest does not rise with ventilations, reposition the head (neutral position in babies); if there is a visible object in the mouth, remove it with a hooked finger ONLY if you see it; continue until professional help or effective breathing.
