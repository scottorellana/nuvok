# Fractures and splinting

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care. Get medical help as soon as possible.

## When to suspect it

- Severe pain that worsens with movement or weight.
- Deformity (compare with the other side), fast swelling, bruising.
- A crack was felt/heard; the part can't be used.
- **Open fracture:** bone broke the skin, or there's a wound over the break.
- Sprain vs fracture unclear? **Treat it as a fracture.**

## What NOT to do

- ❌ DON'T try to "set" the bone or a dislocated joint (you can sever nerves and vessels). Splint it AS IT LIES.
- ❌ DON'T move the person before splinting (unless immediate danger).
- ❌ DON'T push an exposed bone in, don't wash it under pressure: cover with clean moist gauze and control bleeding around it.
- ❌ NO massage, no heat for the first 48 h.

## Steps

1. **Stop any bleeding** first (pressure AROUND exposed bone, not on it).
2. **Check circulation** beyond the injury: fingers/toes warm and pink? Feel your touch? Can wiggle? Note it.
3. **Splint AS IT LIES**, including the joint ABOVE and BELOW the break:
   - **Improvised splint:** board, thick folded cardboard, rolled magazine, straight stick. Pad between splint and skin.
   - Tie with bandages/cloth strips at 3-4 points, **snug but not cutting circulation** — never tie directly OVER the break.
   - **Arm/collarbone/shoulder:** sling from a cloth triangle knotted behind the neck + a band holding the arm to the body.
   - **Finger:** buddy-tape to the healthy neighbor with padding between.
   - **Leg:** long outside splint, or bandage both legs together with padding between (the sound leg is the splint).
   - **Ankle/foot:** L-splint or a rolled pillow tied around.
4. **Re-check circulation** after tying: fingers cold, pale or tingling → loosen the ties.
5. **Cloth-wrapped ice** 15-20 min every hour (never bare on skin) + elevate if possible.
6. **Pain:** over-the-counter painkiller per label. Nothing to eat/drink if surgery is likely (open fracture, big deformity).

## When to get help / transport

- ALWAYS with a suspected fracture: it needs an X-ray.
- URGENT (hours matter): open fracture; cold/blue/pulseless fingers; suspected hip, femur or pelvis (they can bleed liters internally!); any fracture with a pale sweaty patient.
- Pelvis/femur: DON'T move them unless you must; stabilize and wait for help or transport flat on a rigid surface.

## Signs of deterioration

- Cold, blue, numb fingers after splinting → loosen NOW and re-splint.
- Relentlessly growing pain with a rock-hard calf/forearm → surgical emergency: go immediately.
- Pallor + sweat + dizziness → internal shock (guide "Shock").
