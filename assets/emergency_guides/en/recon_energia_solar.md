# Basic Solar Energy

Without the power grid, **a panel + a car battery is a community power station**: phones (the mesh lives on them), night light, and radio — if it is connected with care.

## Critical steps

1. **Gather the neighborhood’s parts:** panels from heaters/garden lights/houses, car batteries (even if they do not start engines, they charge phones for weeks), car inverters (12V→USB/110V), copper wires.
2. **The minimum viable system:** panel → (charge controller if available) → 12V battery → car USB / small inverter. WITHOUT a charge controller: connect the panel only for a few hours of sun and measure that the battery does not go above ~14V if there is a multimeter; a few hours is better than boiling it.
3. **Respect polarity ALWAYS:** red/+ with +, black/– with –. Reversing it kills inverters and can burst the battery. Mark the wires with tape from day 1.
4. **Aim and clean:** panel in direct sun (south if you are in the northern hemisphere), tilted ≈ your latitude, without shadows, NOT even from a cable (a small shadow drops the whole panel), and cleaned of dust every morning.
5. **Prioritize the watts:** 1º mesh phones and flashlights (communication and safety), 2º radio, 3º one communal LED night light. NOTHING with refrigerators/irons on small systems: they kill the battery in an hour.
6. **Care for the lead battery:** do not discharge it fully (if the LED light dims a lot, cut it off), charge it daily, in ventilated shade, NEVER in a closed room where people sleep (it gives off hydrogen gas while charging).
7. **Community charging station with rules:** schedule, one person in charge per shift, labeled phones in line — 20 orderly charged phones are worth more than 3 houses fighting over the outlet.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Connect the panel directly to the phone | Always through a battery/charge controller or car USB |
| Reverse polarity “to see if it works” | Mark wires and check twice |
| Battery charging next to the bed | Ventilated shade, away from sparks and bedrooms |
| Discharge the battery until the light dies | Cut off early: deep discharge kills it |
| Connect the refrigerator to the small system | Only phones/LED/radio; the watts are not enough |
| Panel with “just a little shade, it still charges” | Partial shade = almost dead panel: relocate it |

## How much it yields (honest numbers)

- 50 W panel with good sun ≈ 15-20 phone charges/day.
- Used car battery ≈ 2-4 days of phones+LED without sun.
- Solar garden lamp = emergency nighttime charger for 1 phone (cannibalize its USB if you know how).

## Practical example: Solar Energy

**Situation:** Week 2 without grid power; the neighborhood gathers 2 50 W panels, a pickup battery, and a car inverter.

**Do:** 1. Station in the central courtyard: panels in the sun without shadows, tilted, battery in ventilated shade. 2. Wires marked, panel→battery→inverter/USB. 3. Rules on the board: charging schedule, person in charge per shift, mesh first. 4. Cut off early every afternoon to protect the battery. 5. Clean panels at dawn with a dry rag.

**Avoid:** Do not connect the fridge “for a little while,” do not leave the battery in the sun or in a closed room, do not allow jumpers outside the schedule, do not touch terminals with wet hands.

**Scale up:** If the battery swells, smells like rotten eggs, or boils: disconnect EVERYTHING, ventilate, and remove it — it is an explosion risk; without a battery, panels only by day directly to car USB, and the mesh rations communication to scheduled times.

## Example: Solar Power

**Situation:** Week 2 without the grid; the neighborhood gathers 2 50 W panels, a pickup battery and a car inverter.

**Do:** 1. Station in the central patio: panels in full sun without shadows, tilted, battery in ventilated shade. 2. Marked cables, panel→battery→inverter/USB. 3. Rules on the board: charging schedule, one attendant per shift, mesh phones first. 4. Early cutoff every evening to protect the battery. 5. Panel cleaning at dawn with a dry cloth.

**Avoid:** Don't plug in the fridge "for a bit", don't leave the battery in the sun or in a closed room, don't allow off-schedule jumpers, don't touch terminals with wet hands.

**Escalate:** If the battery swells, smells of rotten eggs or boils: disconnect EVERYTHING, ventilate and retire it — explosion risk; without a battery, panels feed a car USB in direct daylight only, and the mesh rations communication to set hours.
