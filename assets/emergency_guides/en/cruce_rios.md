# Crossing Rivers Safely

Rivers kill more hikers than animals do. **The bravest decision is almost always NOT to cross.**

## Before deciding: assess

- **Depth:** above the knee with current = real danger. Above mid-thigh = do NOT cross.
- **Speed:** toss a stick in; if it moves faster than you walk, the current will knock you down.
- **Bottom:** round rocks and moss = a slide. Sand/gravel = better.
- **Upstream:** is it raining in the mountains? Flash floods arrive in minutes, without warning.
- **Exit:** look at the FAR BANK: it needs an easy exit, not a bluff or thicket.

## Critical steps

1. **Find the best spot, not the closest.** Walk the bank: the WIDEST part of a river is usually the shallowest and slowest. Bends run deep on the outside.
2. **Unclip your pack's hip belt.** Chest and waist unbuckled: if you fall, the pack must come off by itself (waterlogged it drags you under).
3. **Get a strong pole** (chest height). It becomes your "third leg".
4. **Face the current, move sideways.** The water should hit your upstream leg; sidestep diagonally DOWNSTREAM.
5. **Two points planted at all times.** Move ONE support at a time: foot-foot-pole. Shuffle your feet, don't lift them.
6. **In a group: chain up gripping shoulders,** strongest person upstream breaking the current, weakest in the middle.
7. **On the far side: dry off and warm up.** Post-crossing cold causes hypothermia; change clothes and keep moving.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|-----------|-----------|
| Crossing with water above mid-thigh | Find another spot or wait for it to drop |
| Crossing barefoot "to keep boots dry" | Boots on: the bottom cuts and twists ankles |
| Tying yourself to a fixed rope while crossing | The current pins you under against the rope |
| Crossing where the river looks narrow | Narrow = deep and fast; wide = shallow |
| Hopping wet rocks | Ford with technique; wet rocks are ankle traps |
| Crossing a flood surge or suddenly murky water | Wait: flash floods drop within hours |

## If the current takes you

1. **Ditch the pack** immediately.
2. **On your back, feet DOWNSTREAM,** arms out: your feet bounce off rocks, not your head.
3. **Do not try to stand** in deep current: a foot wedges between rocks and the current folds you under (foot-entrapment drowning — the classic fatal mistake).
4. **Swim diagonally** to shore when you feel the current ease.
5. On the bank: **treat hypothermia** — dry clothes, warmth, sugar.

## Children

- One adult per child, child DOWNSTREAM of the adult, gripping the adult's belt.
- If the water passes the child's knee: the child does NOT cross — carry them or don't cross at all.

## Example: Crossing Rivers Safely

**Situation:** The trail ends at a fast knee-deep mountain river.

**Do:** 1. Walk the bank to the WIDEST stretch. 2. Unclip your pack buckles and cut a chest-high pole. 3. Face the current, sidestep diagonally downstream, one support at a time. 4. On the far side: dry clothes and keep moving.

**Avoid:** Don't cross above mid-thigh or in suddenly murky water, don't hop wet rocks, don't tie into a fixed rope, don't go barefoot.

**Escalate:** If someone falls in or the level surges, nobody else enters: reach with arms and poles from the bank, never human chains in current; trigger the mesh SOS.
