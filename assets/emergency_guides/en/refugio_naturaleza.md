# Wilderness Shelter

**Rule of 3s:** you can die in **3 hours** from cold/wind/rain exposure. Shelter comes BEFORE water and food.

## Critical steps

1. **Decide early.** Start your shelter at least 2 hours before sunset. In the dark everything takes three times longer.
2. **Pick the right spot.** Flat, dry ground, protected from wind, near materials (branches, leaves). Check ABOVE you: no dead hanging branches ("widow-makers").
3. **Insulate the ground first.** The ground steals more heat than the air. Lay 20-30 cm of pine boughs, dry leaves or grass BEFORE thinking about a roof.
4. **Build small.** The ideal shelter is barely bigger than your body: your own heat warms it. A big shelter is a cold shelter.
5. **Build a lean-to.** A strong pole between two trees at hip height; branches leaned against it on the windward side; weave thin branches across.
6. **Roof in layers like shingles.** Leaves, bark, grass or pine boughs from the BOTTOM up, each layer overlapping the one below so rain runs off. Minimum thickness: 30 cm (you should see no light through it).
7. **Close the windward side and mark your shelter** with something visible (bright clothing, a cross of branches) so rescuers can spot it.

## Where NOT to build

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|-----------|-----------|
| Bottom of a gully or ravine — cold air pools there and distant rain floods it | Low hillside, partway up |
| Under dead branches or standing dead trees | Check the canopy BEFORE building |
| Exposed hilltop in the wind | In the lee of rocks or dense vegetation |
| Dry stream beds, however inviting | At least 60 m from channels and banks |
| Next to nests, burrows or game trails | Ground with no tracks or droppings |

## Types depending on what you have

### With a tarp or plastic
- Cord between two trees + tarp over it in an A-shape = shelter in 10 minutes.
- Stake the edges with rocks or branches.

### Debris hut (no tools, cold weather)
- Skeleton: one long ridge pole resting on a forked branch (dinosaur-rib shape).
- Side ribs every 30 cm + 60-90 cm of dry leaves ON TOP and INSIDE.
- Crawl in and plug the entrance with a pack or leaves: it is your natural sleeping bag.

### Snow
- NEVER sleep directly on snow: thick layer of pine boughs first.
- A trench roofed with branches and snow on top blocks the wind.

## Keeping warm

- Dry clothes ALWAYS: wet clothing kills. Change or wring out before sleeping.
- Rocks warmed by the fire (NEVER red-hot), wrapped in cloth, inside the shelter.
- Sleep curled up on your insulation when the cold bites.

## Example: Wilderness Shelter

**Situation:** Night is falling in the hills, it's cold, and you won't reach town.

**Do:** 1. Stop NOW: 2 hours of light left — spend them on shelter. 2. Dry low hillside, no dead branches overhead. 3. Gather leaves and boughs: 30 cm of ground bedding first. 4. Small lean-to against the wind, 30 cm layered roof.

**Avoid:** Don't keep walking in the dark, don't sleep on bare ground, don't build in dry stream beds or under dead limbs, don't build big.

**Escalate:** If someone shows hypothermia (uncontrollable shivering, confusion), prioritize shared body heat and fire; trigger the mesh SOS and wait for daylight to move.
