# Heart attack and stroke

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care. Get medical help as soon as possible.

What saves here isn't a maneuver: it's **RECOGNIZING IT NOW and moving NOW**. Every minute kills heart muscle or brain.

## HEART ATTACK — when to suspect it

- **Pain or pressure in the center of the chest** ("like a slab", "squeezing") lasting over 5 minutes or coming and going.
- Pain may spread to the left arm (or both), neck, jaw, back or upper belly.
- Cold sweat, nausea, shortness of breath, sense of doom.
- NOTE: women, diabetics and the elderly may have NO typical pain: sudden crushing fatigue, breathlessness, nausea, vague misery.

## HEART ATTACK — what to do

1. **STOP all activity.** Sit them on the floor or a chair, semi-reclined, knees bent. NO walking "to see if it passes".
2. **Arrange IMMEDIATE transport.** Don't wait it out.  Zero effort from the person (carry them / chair-carry if they must move).
3. **Aspirin:** if NOT allergic and able to swallow, give ONE adult aspirin and have them **CHEW it** before swallowing (works faster). Aspirin only — no other painkillers.
4. Loosen clothing. Speak calmly.
5. **If they collapse and stop breathing → immediate CPR** (guide "CPR for adults") — heart attacks are the #1 cause of arrest.

## STROKE — when to suspect it: the quick test

Ask for these 3 things — if ONE fails, it's a stroke until proven otherwise:
1. **SMILE** → does one side of the face droop?
2. **RAISE BOTH ARMS** → does one drift down or not lift?
3. **REPEAT a phrase** ("the sky is blue") → slurred, strange, or can't?

Also: sudden vision loss, violent dizziness with loss of balance, EXPLOSIVE headache ("worst of my life"), sudden confusion.

**WRITE DOWN THE TIME it started** (or when they were last seen normal): the hospital needs it — key treatments only work in the first hours.

## STROKE — what to do

1. **IMMEDIATE transport.** Stroke doesn't hurt, so people wait — that's the fatal mistake.
2. Lie them with head and shoulders raised (~30°). Vomiting or drooling: on their side.
3. **NOTHING by mouth:** no water, no pills, no "lowering the blood pressure" (swallowing can choke them; dropping pressure can worsen the stroke).
4. Unconscious: recovery position, watch breathing, CPR if it stops.

## What NOT to do (both)

- ❌ DON'T "wait for it to pass" or nap "to recover".
- ❌ DON'T let the person drive themselves.
- ❌ NO home remedies, coffee, alcohol, or someone else's under-the-tongue pill.

## Signs of deterioration

- Loses consciousness, stops breathing → CPR.
- Stroke symptoms "go away on their own" in minutes → STILL go urgently: it was a warning and the big stroke may come within hours.
