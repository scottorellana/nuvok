# Latrines and Sanitation

After the disaster, **sanitation decides whether week 2 brings an epidemic**: cholera does not arrive through bad luck but through feces near water and unwashed hands.

## Critical steps

1. **Trench latrine on DAY 1-2:** trench 30 cm wide × 90 cm deep × as long as possible, 30+ m from all water and DOWNHILL from wells/source. Sturdy board or logs to support the feet, and soil/ash beside it.
2. **The one-cup-of-soil rule:** each use is covered with soil or ash — cuts odor, flies, and the chain of transmission. A bucket with a lid for used paper if the trench is narrow.
3. **MANDATORY handwashing station at the exit:** tippy-tap (hanging container that tilts with the foot) + tied-on soap. Without a handwashing station, the latrine is only a place to gather to get sick.
4. **Privacy and safety:** tarp/sack walls, path light at night, and women’s/children’s latrines closer to the hamlet — an unsafe latrine at night makes people go back to the bush.
5. **Life cycle:** at 30 cm from the top edge, it is closed by covering with 40 cm of compacted soil, marked, and the next trench is opened. Never reopen a closed one.
6. **Trash in its own pit:** organics to compost/pit away from water; what burns, safely burned downwind away from houses; cans/glass crushed in a separate pit. Scattered trash brings rats in days.
7. **Graywater with an outlet:** washing water must drain to a gravel trench/soak pit, not pool — the puddle breeds mosquitoes (dengue) in 72 h.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| "Everyone goes to the bush in the meantime" | Common trench on day 1: the bush contaminates everyone’s water |
| Latrine uphill from the well | 30+ m and ALWAYS downhill |
| No soil/ash next to the trench | Cover each use: flies = epidemic |
| Latrine without handwashing station with soap | Tippy-tap mandatory at the exit |
| Fill the trench to the top | Close at 30 cm from the edge and open another |
| Puddles of wash water everywhere | Drainage to gravel: dengue breeds there |

## Hygiene that sustains the neighborhood

- Hands with soap: before eating/cooking, after latrine use and after touching injured people.
- Water+soap point also in the communal kitchen and where injured people are treated.
- Children: shift of "tippy-tap inspectors" — keeps the system alive and educates them.

## Example: Latrines and Sanitation

**Situation:** Camp of 25 families after the flood; by day 2 people are going to the brush by the river and there are already flies in the communal kitchen.

**Do:** 1. TODAY: two trenches (one family, one women’s/children’s) 40 m from the river, downhill from the camp. 2. Soil+ash with a shovel at each one, sack walls. 3. Two tippy-taps with soap tied on at the exit. 4. Trash pit + controlled burning downwind. 5. Announcement at the meeting: the brush is prohibited and WHY is explained (the river is everyone’s water).

**Avoid:** Do not put the latrine "close by for convenience," do not leave uses uncovered, do not dump wash water onto the path, do not burn trash next to the tarps.

**Escalate:** First case of severe diarrhea or vomiting: isolate their feces (their own latrine if possible), oral rehydration solution (calculator), reinforced chlorine in the water and notice over the mesh; 3+ cases = health emergency — everything boiled, hands monitored, and seek medical evacuation.
