# Basic Solar Energy

Without the electrical grid, **a panel + a car battery is a community power station**: phones (the mesh lives on them), nighttime light, and radio — if it is connected intelligently.

## Critical steps

1. **Gather the neighborhood's parts:** panels from heaters/garden lights/houses, car batteries (even if they do not start engines, they charge phones for weeks), car inverters (12V→USB/110V), copper wires.
2. **The minimum viable system:** panel → (charge controller if available) → 12V battery → car USB / small inverter. WITHOUT a charge controller: connect the panel for only a few hours of sun and measure that the battery does not go above ~14V if there is a multimeter; a few hours is better than boiling it.
3. **Respect polarity ALWAYS:** red/+ with +, black/– with –. Reversing it kills inverters and can burst the battery. Mark the cables with tape from day 1.
4. **Aim and clean:** panel in direct sun (south if you are in the northern hemisphere), tilted ≈ your latitude, with no shadows, NOT EVEN from a cable (one small shadow brings down the entire panel), and cleaned of dust every morning.
5. **Prioritize the watts:** 1º mesh phones and flashlights (communication and safety), 2º radio, 3º one communal nighttime LED light. NOTHING like refrigerators/irons on small systems: they kill the battery in an hour.
6. **Take care of the lead-acid battery:** do not deep-discharge it (if the LED light loses a lot of brightness, cut it off), charge it daily, in ventilated shade, NEVER in a closed room where people sleep (it gives off hydrogen gas when charging).
7. **Community charging station with rules:** schedule, one person in charge per shift, labeled phones in line — 20 phones charged in an orderly way are worth more than 3 houses fighting over the outlet.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Connecting the panel directly to the phone | Always through a battery/charge controller or car USB |
| Reversing polarity "to see if it works" | Mark cables and verify twice |
| Battery charging next to the bed | Ventilated shade, away from sparks and bedrooms |
| Discharging the battery until the light dies | Cut it off early: deep discharge kills it |
| Connecting the refrigerator to the small system | Only phones/LED/radio; the watts are not enough |
| Panel with a "little shade that still charges" | Partial shade = nearly dead panel: relocate it |

## How much it yields (honest numbers)

- Panel 50 W with good sun ≈ 15-20 phone charges/day.
- Used car battery ≈ 2-4 days of phones+LED without sun.
- Solar garden lamp = emergency nighttime charger for 1 phone (cannibalize its USB if you know how).

## Example: Solar Energy

**Situation:** Week 2 without grid power; the neighborhood gathers 2 panels of 50 W, a pickup battery, and a car inverter.

**Do:** 1. Station in the central yard: panels in the sun with no shadows, tilted, battery in ventilated shade. 2. Cables marked, panel→battery→inverter/USB. 3. Rules on the board: charging schedule, person in charge per shift, mesh first. 4. Cut off early every afternoon to protect the battery. 5. Clean panels at dawn with a dry cloth.

**Avoid:** Do not connect the fridge "for a little while," do not leave the battery in the sun or in a closed room, do not allow jumpers outside scheduled hours, do not touch terminals with wet hands.

**Escalate:** If the battery swells, smells like rotten eggs, or boils: disconnect EVERYTHING, ventilate, and remove it — it is an explosion risk; without a battery, panels only during the day directly to car USB, and the mesh rations communication to scheduled times.
