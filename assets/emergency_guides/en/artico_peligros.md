# Ice and Snow Hazards

Ice lies: **what held yesterday does not hold today**, the blizzard erases the world in minutes and frostbite bites without pain.

## Critical steps

1. **Read the ice before stepping on it:** transparent/bluish = strong; milky white = half strength; gray/dark or snow-covered = NO. Minimums on NEW transparent ice: 10 cm on foot, 20 cm for a snowmobile. Strike with the pole: hollow sound = back up.
2. **Distrust the eternal weak points:** river inlets/outlets, currents under the lake, next to rocks and docks, ice with bubbles or wet cracks. The center of the lake is no guarantee.
3. **Cross spread out and with a plan:** one at a time or 10+ m apart, rope ready, backpack with buckles loose, and ice picks (or two screwdrivers/wrenches) hung around the neck.
4. **If you fall through the ice:** control the gasping (1 min), face THE WAY YOU CAME (that ice held you), arms over the edge, kick horizontally and dig in the picks to slide out like a seal. Do NOT stand up: ROLL away from the hole.
5. **Out of the water: wet clothes get changed or rolled in snow** (dry snow absorbs water from clothing) and shelter + fire NOW — you have minutes of useful hands (hypothermia guide).
6. **Blizzard: stop BEFORE getting lost.** Falling visibility = immediate shelter (cold guide). Walking in the white ends in circles, crevasses or open water.
7. **Watch for frostbite in pairs:** check your partner's face every 15 min in strong wind (white waxy patches on nose/cheeks = frostbite starting: warm hand on top, cover better, no rubbing).

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Crossing snow-covered ice "it looks firm" | Snow hides and weakens: probe or go around |
| Crossing in a tight group | One at a time or far apart, rope ready |
| Standing up right after getting out of the water | ROLL away from the hole, then crawl |
| Drying off by "toughing it out" in wet clothes | Change or roll in dry snow + fire NOW |
| Rubbing your partner's white cheeks | Gentle heat without friction and cover |
| Walking the blizzard "it is only 2 km" | Shelter: the white erases even your tracks |

## Homemade ice picks

1. Two large nails/screwdrivers/pointed wrenches.
2. Handles that fit your fist (wrapped cloth).
3. A cord joining them run inside your sleeves: they hang from each wrist, ready without searching.

## Example: Ice Hazards

**Situation:** To cut toward the village you must cross a frozen lake; it snowed last night and the ice cannot be seen.

**Do:** 1. Honest decision: fresh snow on top = unreadable ice — go around the shore even if it takes 2 more hours. 2. If a stretch is unavoidable: probe the snow until you see transparent ice 10+ cm. 3. One at a time, spread out, picks at the neck, buckles loose. 4. Hollow sound or milky ice: turn back along your own tracks. 5. On the far side, check faces and hands.

**Avoid:** Do not cross together, do not trust others' old tracks, do not continue if the ice gives a dry "crack", do not take off the picks because "they get in the way".

**Escalate:** If someone falls through: do NOT run to the hole — approach crawling to spread your weight, reach with a rope/branch/outstretched coat, and pull them out toward WHERE THEY CAME FROM; then it is a race against hypothermia: shelter, dry clothes, heat and the hypothermia guide with the mesh SOS.
