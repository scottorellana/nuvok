# Mountain Dangers

The mountain punishes bad timing: **the afternoon lightning, the snow that lets go and the altitude that poisons** all give warning first — you have to know how to read them.

## Critical steps

1. **Thunderstorm: get down BEFORE noon.** In the mountains storms build in the afternoon: summit reached by midday or turn back. If your hair stands on end or the air buzzes: lightning is IMMINENT.
2. **Lightning position.** Away from summits, lone trees, cables and ridges: crouch on your backpack (insulation), feet together, group SPREAD OUT 15 m apart — never hugging or lying down.
3. **Avalanche: avoid loaded 30-45° slopes.** Fresh snow (24-48 h after a snowfall), slabs that make a hollow "whumpf," wind-loaded lee slopes: go around via the ridge or the flat valley. Cross ONE at a time if there is no option.
4. **If the avalanche takes you:** release poles/big pack, "swim" toward the edge, and as it slows: hand in front of your face to create an air pocket and one arm reaching up.
5. **Altitude sickness: the only cure is DESCENT.** Headache + nausea + fatigue above 2,500 m = stop, hydrate, climb no higher. Confusion, staggering or breathlessness at rest = descend NOW even at night, it kills within hours.
6. **Rockfall:** mornings are the safe window — the ice that welds the rocks melts with the sun. In narrow gullies: fast, one at a time, without stopping, eyes uphill. The shout "ROCK!" = press against the wall, do not look up without a helmet.
7. **The descent kills more than the climb:** most accidents happen on the way down, tired and rushing. Descend focused, hydrated and with daylight.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Going for the summit with storm clouds building | Summit before noon or retreat |
| Sheltering from lightning under the tallest tree | Crouch in a low area, group spread out |
| Crossing a loaded snow slope as a group | One at a time, spaced out, or go around |
| Ignoring a headache at altitude | Stop, hydrate; if it worsens: DESCEND |
| "Toughing out" altitude sickness by sleeping higher | Always sleep at or below the day's high point |
| Running downhill out of exhaustion | 70% of accidents happen on the descent |

## Crossing hard snow and scree

- Morning hard snow: kick steps, pole planted uphill, and ALWAYS ask yourself "if I slip, where do I end up?"
- Scree (loose talus): step on the LARGE settled rocks, move diagonally, group in zigzag never in a vertical line (one drops rocks onto another).

## Example: Mountain Dangers

**Situation:** You are halfway up at 11 a.m.; black clouds are building to the west and above 3,000 m one companion has a headache and nausea.

**Do:** 1. Immediate decision: the summit is over — today the mountain said no. 2. Descend by the known route BEFORE the storm. 3. The one with altitude sickness goes down first with a companion, hydrating. 4. If the storm catches you: crouch on packs, spread out, away from ridges. 5. Regroup below and reassess symptoms.

**Avoid:** Do not "race to summit before the rain," do not shelter under the only tree, do not leave the sick one alone, do not descend through the rock gully with the storm overhead.

**Escalate:** If the one with altitude sickness staggers or gets confused, it is cerebral edema (HACE): IMMEDIATE descent, carrying them if needed + mesh SOS; if lightning strikes someone, they are safe to touch — CPR immediately (CPR guide).
