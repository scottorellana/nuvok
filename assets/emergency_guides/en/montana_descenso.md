# Getting Lost and Descending in the Mountains

Lost in the mountains, the instinct to "get down any way you can" kills: **ravines narrow, waterfalls are invisible from above, and fog erases the world**.

## Critical steps

1. **STOP the moment you are unsure.** The STOP rule: Stop, Take a breath, Observe, Plan. Walking while lost multiplies the error; rescuers search from your last known point.
2. **Retrace your steps if you can.** The last point where you KNEW where you were is gold: go back there even if it means climbing, even if it hurts.
3. **Fog or night = camp.** Moving without visibility in the mountains is Russian roulette with ravines. Shelter (cold guide) and wait: mountain fog usually lifts at dawn.
4. **To descend: ridge or spur, NEVER the ravine.** Watercourses box into waterfalls and rock walls. Broad ridges and spurs descend gradually and with visibility.
5. **Follow water ONLY at a distance.** A river below leads to people, yes — but walk along the WIDE slope above the valley, not inside the gorge.
6. **Mark your trail.** Stone cairns, crossed branches, arrows: if you have to go back or they come looking, your trail speaks.
7. **Test every step on steep ground.** Three points of contact on a strong slope, face the slope when climbing down, and when in doubt: take the long way around — a broken ankle here changes everything.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| "Go down the ravine, the water comes out somewhere" | Ridges and broad spurs; water at a distance |
| Walking in thick fog or at night | Shelter and wait for visibility |
| Pushing "just a bit more" when in doubt | STOP: pause, think, return to the known point |
| Climbing down with your back to the wall | Face the slope, three points of contact |
| Splitting up "to explore in two directions" | Group TOGETHER always; at most within sight |
| Keeping quiet about doubt out of embarrassment | Say it right away: everyone rechecks the bearing |

## Signals for rescuers

- 3 of anything = distress: 3 whistle blasts, 3 flashes, 3 fires.
- Open area near the shelter with a large high-contrast signal (clothing, branches on snow).
- A whistle carries farther than your voice and does not exhaust you (rescue signals guide).

## Example: Lost in the Mountains

**Situation:** The trail disappeared under fog on the way down; for an hour you "think" you are on track and nothing looks familiar.

**Do:** 1. STOP where you are: not one more step downhill. 2. Can you retrace your ascent? Return to the last certain landmark even if it takes an hour. 3. If the fog closes in: shelter at mid-slope and wait for dawn. 4. With visibility: choose the broad spur with a gentle slope, marking cairns. 5. Mesh SOS active and whistle every few minutes in clearings.

**Avoid:** Do not go down the gully "that sounds like water," do not walk at night, do not improvise shortcuts down steep slopes, do not drain the phone endlessly hunting for signal (beacon mode).

**Escalate:** If someone falls and cannot walk: do NOT move them downhill on a slope — stabilize (fractures/trauma guide), shelter on the spot, and the mesh + concentrated signals become your only mission.
