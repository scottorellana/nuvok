# Desert Heat and Shelter

The desert kills twice: **heat at midday and cold before dawn**. Your shelter and your schedule are your survival.

## Critical steps

1. **Invert your day.** Move ONLY at dawn, dusk and (with moonlight) at night. From 10 to 16 h: total shade and stillness. This is the rule that saves the most lives.
2. **Clothes stay ON.** Loose, light-colored, long-sleeved clothing + covered head and neck: fabric blocks the sun and traps sweat to cool you. Taking your shirt off dehydrates you faster and burns your skin.
3. **Get off the ground.** At midday the ground is 15-20 °C hotter than the air 30 cm up. Lie on your pack, branches or stacked rocks, never directly on the sand.
4. **DOUBLE roof with an air gap.** Two layers of cloth/tarp separated by 20-30 cm cut up to 15 °C versus direct sun: a single layer only gives warm shade.
5. **Use what exists.** The north-facing shade of a boulder, a rock overhang or your vehicle (NOT underneath: metal radiates; beside it, on the shaded side) beat anything you can build.
6. **Prepare the night BEFORE sunset.** The desert can drop from 35 °C to below 5 °C: gather firewood in daylight, close the shelter on the wind side and keep a dry layer for sleeping.
7. **Watch the group for heat stroke.** Hot, dry skin with NO sweat, confusion, stumbling gait: immediate shade, wet the clothes, fan — and switch to the heat stroke guide.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|-----------|-----------|
| Walking at midday "to make progress" | Move at dawn/dusk/night |
| Stripping off clothes because of heat | Loose light clothes ON, head covered |
| Lying directly on hot sand | 30 cm of separation from the ground |
| Sheltering INSIDE a vehicle in the sun | Shade beside the vehicle, ventilated |
| Ignoring the night ("it's the desert, it's hot") | Prepare warmth and fire before sunset |
| Waiting for thirst during effort | Small regular sips while moving |

## Emergency shelter in 20 minutes

1. Find a natural depression or the shaded side of a big rock.
2. Anchor a tarp/blanket from a high rock to two low stakes (lean-to against the afternoon sun).
3. Second layer (spare shirt, another tarp) 20-30 cm ABOVE the first.
4. Floor: packs, branches or flat stones — never bare sand.
5. Opening toward the breeze; never sealed like an oven.

## The desert night

- Real cold: hypothermia is possible after a 40 °C day.
- Small steady fire > bonfire; firewood is scarce.
- Rocks warmed by the fire, wrapped in cloth, inside the shelter.
- Check boots and clothes at dawn: scorpions seek your warmth (hazards guide).

## Example: Heat and Shelter

**Situation:** Lost on a hike in arid terrain; it's 11 a.m., the sun is vertical and no town in sight.

**Do:** 1. STOP now: find the north shade of a boulder. 2. Rig a double roof with your tarp and spare shirt, body off the ground. 3. Drink regular sips and stay still until 4-5 p.m. 4. At dusk: gather firewood, prepare the night and move only with usable light. 5. Trigger the mesh SOS from the shade.

**Avoid:** Don't keep walking at midday, don't take off your shirt, don't sit in an unventilated car/cave, don't leave the night for improvisation.

**Escalate:** If someone stops sweating with hot dry skin, or gets confused: it's heat stroke — aggressive cooling NOW (water on clothes + fanning) and switch to the heat stroke guide; if seizures start, seizure guide.
