# Survival Food

## Golden Rule

**"If you don't know it, DON'T eat it"**

- 90% of wild plants are safe
- The remaining 10% can kill you

## Insects (edible)

### Safe (cook first)
- Crickets, grasshoppers
- Ants (especially leaf-cutter)
- Agave worms
- Cicadas

### DO NOT eat
- Fuzzy caterpillars
- Brightly colored insects
- Bees/wasps with stinger
- Decomposing dead insects

## Fish

### How to clean
1. Remove innards immediately
2. Remove head and tail
3. Wash with clean water
4. Cook thoroughly

### Toxic fish (don't eat)
- Puffer fish
- Some tropical fish species

## Mammal Meat

### Cleaning technique
1. Bleed completely
2. Remove skin and innards
3. Discard organs except heart/liver
4. Cook well (no rare)

### Safe animals
- Small game: rabbit, hare, squirrel
- Rodents: rats, mice
- Deer (if available)

### Dangerous animals
- Animals dead from disease
- Predators (foxes, raccoons)
- Animals with foam at mouth

## Safe Plants

### Edible fruits
- Blackberries, wild strawberries
- Wild apple (without seeds)
- Grapes, figs

### Vegetables
- Fern stems (cook 2x)
- Thistle roots
- Lily tubers

### DO NOT eat
- Plants with white latex
- Red or black berries in clusters
- Mushrooms (unless you're an expert)
- Bulbous plants similar to onion

## Purification

### Always cook
- All meat: 15+ minutes
- Fish: until flesh separates
- Insects: until they crackle

### When there's no fire
- Fish: clean and eat raw if fresh
- Insects: safe raw (except larvae)
- Fruits: only ones you know

## Preservation

### Without refrigeration
- Dry in sun (meat + fish)
- Smoke if you have smoke
- Salt if you have salt

### Approximate shelf life
- Dried meat: 1 week
- Dried fish: 3-5 days
- Tubers: several weeks
- Dried fruits: 1 month

## Example practice: Survival Food

**Situation:** Power is out for several days and you need to ration food for four people.

**Do:** 1. Pause for 30 seconds and name the main danger. 2. Gather water, warmth, light, communication, and a safe route. 3. Take the simplest action that reduces risk right now. 4. Record what you did in notes/checklists so another person can continue.

**Avoid:** Do not drain battery without a plan, do not cross moving water, do not eat unknown plants, do not split the group, and do not wait until dark to build shelter.

**Escalate:** Change plans if weather worsens, body temperature drops, safe water is running out, vulnerable people are present, or you lose orientation.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who is responsible next. This is a guided practice example; adapt it to your environment and prioritize professional help when available.
