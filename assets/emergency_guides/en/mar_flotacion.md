# Float and Survive at Sea

In the water you do not survive by swimming: **you survive by floating and conserving heat**. Swimming exhausts and chills you; floating is a plan.

## Critical steps

1. **Float first, do not swim.** Cold water shock makes you gasp for 1-2 minutes: float on your back, breathe, let the panic pass BEFORE deciding anything.
2. **Grab EVERYTHING that floats.** Coolers, jugs, boards, wreckage from the boat: between your body and the bottom of the sea, any float is worth more than your strength.
3. **HELP position against the cold.** With a life jacket or float: knees to chest, arms crossed hugging the jacket, stay still. Cuts heat loss by up to 50%. In a group: huddle in a circle holding each other, children in the center.
4. **Clothes stay ON.** The undressing myth kills: clothing traps water your body warms and gives brief flotation. Only ditch what sinks you (heavy boots if you cannot float).
5. **Pants as a float.** Take them off only if you are floating stable: knot each ankle, seal at the waist by filling them with air over your head. Refill every few minutes.
6. **Swim ONLY with a clear goal.** Shore visible and reachable, current in your favor, something floating nearby? Swim backstroke/breaststroke to save energy. If not: stay still and float — rescuers spot a fixed point more easily.
7. **Rip current: parallel to the beach.** If the sea drags you out, do NOT fight it head-on: swim parallel to the coast until you exit the channel, then return at a diagonal.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Swimming immediately toward a distant shore | Float, assess, decide calmly |
| Undressing "to swim better" | Clothes on: they warm you and float |
| Fighting the rip current head-on | Parallel to the beach and out of the channel |
| Drinking seawater "just a little" | NEVER: it dehydrates twice as fast and clouds judgment |
| Thrashing and shouting nonstop | Signal with shiny objects/clothing when boats are in sight |
| Leaving the group or the wreckage | Wreckage floats and is SEEN from the air |

## Conserving heat in the water

- Water steals heat 25× faster than air: hypothermia decides how much time you have.
- Do not swim to "warm up": move as little as possible, tuck your body in.
- Get as much of your body as you can out of the water onto any float: half your body out doubles your time.

## Example: Floating at Sea

**Situation:** The boat capsized 500 m from the coast; there are waves and the current is pulling you away from the beach.

**Do:** 1. Float on your back until you control your breathing. 2. Hold onto the capsized hull with the others: it is your island and your signal. 3. Group huddle position, children in the center. 4. If the current is a rip, the strongest swimmer goes parallel to the beach to seek help; everyone else stays on the hull. 5. Flash your phone/metal at any boat.

**Avoid:** Do not all swim toward the beach against the current, do not undress, do not drink seawater, do not leave the hull.

**Escalate:** With uncontrollable shivering or confusion (hypothermia), lift the person onto the hull as far out of the water as possible and cover them; once ashore, switch to the hypothermia guide — post-rescue collapse is real.
