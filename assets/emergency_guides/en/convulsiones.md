# Seizures

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care. Get medical help as soon as possible.

## When to suspect it

- Falls unconscious, body stiffens, then rhythmic jerking of arms and legs.
- Eyes rolled back, clenched jaw, sometimes foamy saliva or loss of urine.
- Usually lasts 1-3 minutes, followed by confusion and deep sleepiness (normal).

## What NOT to do

- ❌ DON'T put ANYTHING in their mouth (no spoon, cloth or fingers: swallowing the tongue is a MYTH — but they can bite your finger off or break their teeth).
- ❌ DON'T hold them down or try to stop the jerking (you can fracture them).
- ❌ NO water, food or pills until FULLY awake.
- ❌ DON'T leave afterwards: the confusion phase is part of the seizure.

## Steps

1. **Check the time.** Duration matters enormously.
2. **Protect the head:** something soft underneath (sweater, your hand). Push away furniture, rocks, hard objects.
3. Loosen anything around the neck. Remove glasses.
4. **Let the seizure run.** Your job is preventing injury, not stopping the fit.
5. When jerking stops: **roll them onto their SIDE** (recovery position) so saliva drains and they breathe well. Wipe the mouth outside.
6. Stay until fully awake. Keep it simple: "You had a seizure. You're OK. I'm with you." Expect 10-30 min of confusion.
7. Check for injuries (head, bitten tongue, shoulder).

## Special cases

- **In water:** keep the head above water, pull them out when it stops, treat as drowning (check breathing).
- **Pregnant, diabetic, or first seizure ever:** urgent transport always.
- **Small child with high fever (febrile seizure):** onto the side, remove extra clothing, do NOT dunk in cold water; always see a doctor to rule out serious causes — most leave no damage.

## When to get help / transport — URGENT if:

- Lasts **more than 5 minutes** (watch the clock — it feels eternal, count real minutes).
- Seizures chain together without waking in between.
- Not starting to respond 10-15 minutes after it ends.
- Head was hit in the fall, pregnancy, diabetes, or FIRST-ever seizure.
- Happened in water.
- Breathing stays difficult or lips stay blue.

## Signs of deterioration

- Very noisy or absent breathing afterwards → open the airway (on the side, chin up); not breathing → CPR.
- Not waking after 15 minutes → go now.

## Example practice: Seizures (epileptic fit)

**Situation:** A person falls and has shaking movements while a table, glasses, and cables are nearby.

**Do:** 1. Make the scene safe and assign one specific person to call emergency services. 2. Follow the large steps in this guide in order instead of jumping to the most dramatic action. 3. Use gloves, barriers, or clean cloth if available. 4. Recheck every minute: breathing, responsiveness, bleeding, temperature, or worsening signs.

**Avoid:** Do not improvise advanced procedures, do not move the neck/spine if injury is suspected, do not give food or drink to a confused person, and do not delay calling because you are searching for perfect equipment.

**Escalate:** Call or evacuate immediately if breathing is not normal, chest pain appears, bleeding does not stop, confusion worsens, a burn is large, poisoning is possible, pregnancy is advanced, or there are multiple victims.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who is responsible next. This is a guided practice example; adapt it to your environment and prioritize professional help when available.
