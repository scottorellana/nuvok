# Extreme Cold and Snow Shelter

In deep sub-zero, **snow stops being the enemy: it is your building material**. A snow cave holds ~0 °C inside with -30 °C outside.

## Critical steps

1. **Shelter BEFORE you are in trouble.** In a whiteout or at -20 °C, the useful window of working hands is short: decide on shelter with energy and light to spare, not at the limit.
2. **Snow trench (the fastest):** a ditch the size of your body + 40 cm, roof of blocks/skis/branches + snow on top, low entrance blocked with the backpack. 30-60 min of work.
3. **Cave in a compact slope (the warmest):** a tunnel that CLIMBS to a domed chamber; the sleeping platform HIGHER than the entrance (cold drains down and out). Walls 30+ cm.
4. **VENT HOLE or you do not wake up.** A fist-sized hole with a stick through the roof, and the stick gets moved every so often to keep it open. CO₂ in a sealed shelter kills you in your sleep.
5. **Work WITHOUT sweating.** Building while sweating = wet clothes = delayed hypothermia. Take off layers to work, put them back on when you finish. Shake ALL the snow off your clothes before entering.
6. **Insulate from the ground three times over:** a mattress of branches/backpack/coiled rope — the ice under your body steals more than the air. Boots and gloves are NEVER slept in if damp: to the chest, inside the coat.
7. **Frostbite: body heat, not fire.** White, hard fingers/nose/ears: your own or a partner's armpit/groin, WITHOUT rubbing (the crystals cut the tissue). NEVER thaw if it can refreeze on the march.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Sealing the shelter airtight "against the cold" | Vent hole ALWAYS open and watched |
| Building at full sweat with the coat on | Layers off to work; dry ones on when done |
| Rubbing frozen skin with snow | Direct body heat, no friction |
| Sleeping with damp boots on | To the chest: your heat dries them little by little |
| A big shelter "to be comfortable" | Small and low: your heat must fill it |
| Leaving the shelter in a whiteout to get your bearings | Anchored guide rope or wait inside |

## Whiteout

- If the world blurs out: shelter or stay put. Walking in the white = circles and crevasses.
- Rope anchored to the shelter for any exit (a latrine 5 steps away gets lost).
- Mark the shelter HIGH (pole + cloth): snow buries the entrance while you sleep.

## Example: Extreme Cold

**Situation:** Snowmobile broken down at -25 °C; the wind is picking up and the village is 20 km away.

**Do:** 1. Decision NOW: nobody walks 20 km in a blizzard — shelter next to the machine (it is a visible signal). 2. Trench on the protected side, roof with the seat/tarp + snow. 3. Layers off while you dig; shake off and put on everything dry when entering. 4. Vent hole with a stick + triple insulation below. 5. Mesh SOS beacon and wait: they will search where the machine is.

**Avoid:** Do not try to "get there quickly" on foot, do not seal the roof, do not fall asleep in damp clothes, do not leave the machine (your visible point).

**Escalate:** If someone stops shivering, slurs their words or wants to "sleep a while" out in the open: severe hypothermia — hypothermia guide NOW, skin-to-skin heat inside the shelter, and no decision of theirs counts until they come back to their senses.
