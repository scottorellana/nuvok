# Dangers of the Sea and the Coast

The sea does not forgive ignorance: **rip currents kill more people than sharks**, the tide traps you, and what you step on can poison you.

## Critical steps

1. **Read the rip current BEFORE going in.** A channel of strangely calm water between wave zones, murky/foamy water flowing out to sea: do NOT go in there. If it catches you: parallel to the beach, never head-on.
2. **Respect the tide.** Before walking along the base of a cliff or out to a tide pool, look at the algae/moisture line (the water rises that far). Rising tide + cliff = deadly trap: plan your return with hours to spare.
3. **Shuffle your feet as you enter.** A buried stingray stings the foot that STEPS on it, not the one that nudges it: the "stingray shuffle" scares it away.
4. **Do not touch what you do not know.** Jellyfish (even stranded, "dead" ones sting), cone snails, small bright octopuses, sea urchins: the rule is simple — beautiful and still = probably venomous.
5. **Bleeding? Get out of the water.** For any bleeding wound, get out: blood draws predators and seawater infects. Wash with fresh water, cover, and follow the wound guide.
6. **Jellyfish sting: SEA water, not fresh.** Rinse with seawater (fresh water discharges more venom), scrape off tentacles with the edge of a card/flat knife, apply local heat (hot water 20-45 min) for most; see the bites guide.
7. **Reef and rocks with surf: never turn your back on the sea.** "Sneaker waves" sweep people off the rocks every year: watch the pattern for 10 minutes before approaching.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Swimming head-on against the rip | Parallel to the beach, return at a diagonal |
| Exploring the cliff base on a rising tide | Check the tide line and return with time to spare |
| Urinating on a jellyfish sting | SEA water + remove tentacles + heat |
| Walking barefoot on reef/tide pools | Footwear always; shuffle your feet in the sand |
| Collecting cone snails/octopuses/jellyfish "as souvenirs" | Do not touch unknown creatures, even dead ones |
| Turning your back on the surf on the rocks | Watch the pattern and keep an escape route |

## Sharks (the real risk is minimal)

- Avoid swimming at dawn/dusk, with bleeding wounds, or among jumping bait fish.
- If you see one: slow movements, stay vertical, no splashing, move toward shore without taking your eyes off it.
- Strike the snout/eyes/gills ONLY if there is contact — there almost never is.

## Example: Dangers of the Sea

**Situation:** You are walking along the beach toward a rocky point to fish; the passage under the cliff is dry and the afternoon is wearing on.

**Do:** 1. Look at the algae line on the cliff: if the passage sits below it, the tide will cover it. 2. Confirm whether the tide is rising or falling (watch a rock for half an hour). 3. Cross ONLY on a falling tide and return before it turns. 4. Fish facing the sea, never with your back to it. 5. Footwear on at all times.

**Avoid:** Do not cross "real quick" on a rising tide, do not step into pools without looking, do not touch stranded jellyfish, do not stand at the edge of the breaking surf.

**Escalate:** If the tide traps you against the cliff: find the highest reachable point NOW and trigger the mesh SOS — climbing 3 m buys you hours; swimming out through surf against rocks is the last resort, and only with a float.
