# Traveling by Following the River

"Follow the river and you will reach people" is **half true**: the river finds towns, but its immediate bank is among the most dangerous places to walk.

## Critical steps

1. **First decide whether to move.** If people are searching for you, a fixed point with signals is worth more than kilometers. Move only if NOBODY expects you, you are healthy and you have days of light.
2. **The river is your compass, not your trail.** Walk the hillside or HIGH terrace of the valley, with the river in sight or earshot — not the bank itself (slippery, undercut, boxed in).
3. **Downstream = civilization (almost always).** Settlements follow the water. Downstream the valleys open and roads, fences, crops appear: follow them at the first sign.
4. **Cross tributaries at their WIDE part** and in the morning (river crossing guide). A deep tributary may force you upstream a stretch: accept it, do not force it.
5. **Canyon closing in = climb and go around.** If the walls narrow and the bank disappears, do NOT keep going inside hopping rocks: turn back, climb to the ridge and go around above.
6. **Survivor's pace:** 4-5 useful hours per day, camp ready 2 h before dark (and HIGH, flood guide), feet dry and checked every night.
7. **Leave a trail for rescuers:** cairns, stone arrows, initials and date scratched where you camp. If they search by air: a big contrast signal in every clearing.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Walking the stones of the bank itself | High terrace/hillside with the river in sight |
| Entering the canyon "because the river continues" | Climb and go around; the canyon traps you |
| Walking until the last minute of light | Camp set 2 h before, up high |
| Leaving the safe point when they are searching for you | Staying and signaling is worth more than advancing |
| Crossing the river "to cut the bend" | Every crossing is a risk: only if essential |
| Walking on wet feet day after day | Dry and check feet every night (trench foot disables) |

## Reading the valley

- Valley OPENING, water calming: people near.
- Trash, cans, machete cuts, cattle trails: follow them — livestock walk down to drink by the easy route.
- Smoke at dusk, roosters at dawn, dogs: listen in silence 2 minutes every hour.

## Example: Traveling by the River

**Situation:** Third day lost; nobody knows your route, you are unhurt and you found a river heading down with purpose.

**Do:** 1. Confirm it: nobody is searching for you here — time to move. 2. Climb to the valley terrace and walk downstream with the water within earshot. 3. Mark cairns and scratch the date at every high camp. 4. At a canyon: ridge and around. 5. At the first cattle trail or fence, follow it — the house is near.

**Avoid:** Do not walk the wet bank, do not force canyons or crossings, do not walk at night, do not stop signaling to move faster.

**Escalate:** If you get injured en route: return to "fixed point" mode — visible high shelter, triple signals, mesh SOS and beacon; advancing while limping turns a rescue of days into a tragedy.
