# Water and Resources in the City

The city without services **hides hundreds of liters within your reach** — in tanks, pipes and water heaters. Knowing where and in what order is worth more than going out to search.

## Critical steps

1. **When the water cuts out: fill EVERYTHING now.** In the first minutes there is still pressure: tubs, pots, bottles. Then close the main shutoff valve (it keeps the clean water in your pipes from draining away or getting contaminated back).
2. **Your house holds hidden water:** the water heater (40-60 L: bottom valve, open a hot tap above so air enters), the pipes (open the LOWEST tap in the house), the toilet reservoir (the TANK, not the bowl — purify it) and the ice in the freezer.
3. **Order of urban sources:** 1) bottled, 2) water heaters and building tanks, 3) cisterns/rainwater tanks, 4) pools and fountains ONLY for hygiene or disinfected with judgment (unknown chemistry), 5) urban river: last resort and with the full purification ladder.
4. **Urban rain counts:** tarps and containers in patios and on rooftops; discard the first minutes (it washes the roof) and disinfect.
5. **Disinfect EVERYTHING doubtful:** boil 1 min or chlorine (the calculator). "Clear" urban water can carry whatever the broken pipe picked up from the ground.
6. **Resources without looting: the neighborhood network.** Organize with neighbors WHAT each one has (community organization guide): the building's tank + Mrs. X's garden + the neighbor's pharmacy are worth more shared than fought over.
7. **Ration at 3-4 L per person/day** (drinking + minimal cooking): with that, a family of 4 lives off a 50 L water heater for about 3-4 days — time to organize more.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|----------|------------|
| Drinking from the toilet bowl | From the TANK (reservoir) and disinfected |
| Drinking "chlorinated" pool water | Unknown accumulated chemistry: hygiene only |
| Leaving the main valve open after the cut | Close it: it protects the water in your pipes |
| Draining the heater without opening a tap above | Air enters from above or nothing comes out |
| Hoarding behind the neighborhood's back | Neighbor network: safety and resources last longer |
| Drinking the "clear bit" from the broken pipe untreated | Boil or chlorinate ALWAYS after a cut |

## Hygiene with counted water

- Handwashing with a jug and soap: 0.5 L well used > 3 L running.
- Dish/laundry rinse water is reused for the toilet (bucket straight into the bowl).
- No water for the toilet: double bag in the bowl + soil/ash per use (sanitation guide).

## Example: Urban Water

**Situation:** Earthquake: no power or running water in the whole neighborhood, and your family has 6 small bottles.

**Do:** 1. Open the lowest tap and fill pots with what is left in the pipes; close the main valve. 2. Drain the water heater (bottom valve + hot tap open): +50 L. 3. Salvage the toilet tank and the ice. 4. Tarp in the patio in case it rains. 5. Meet with the neighbors: inventory and watch shifts for the shared tank.

**Avoid:** Do not spend drinking water on dishes, do not drink from the building's pool, do not let everyone "grab what they can" from the shared tank, do not drink without disinfecting.

**Escalate:** If diarrhea appears in several homes, the shared water is contaminated: suspend it, switch everyone to boiled water + oral rehydration (the calculator) and mark it; the mesh carries the notice to the whole neighborhood.
