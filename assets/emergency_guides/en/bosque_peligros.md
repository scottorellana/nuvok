# Forest Dangers

In the forest almost nothing hunts you: **most accidents are self-inflicted** — where you step, where you put your hand, and where you sleep.

## Critical steps

1. **Watch where you step and grab.** 90% of snakebites hit feet and hands: step ON logs (never over without looking), never reach into holes, under rocks, or into leaf litter.
2. **Noise = your shield.** Walk with natural noise: almost every animal avoids you if it hears you coming. The danger is surprising them.
3. **Shake EVERYTHING before using it.** Boots, clothes and pack in the morning: scorpions and spiders seek warmth at night.
4. **Check the ceiling before sheltering.** Dead hanging branches ("widow-makers"), wasp nests, ant colonies in the tree — a branch kills more people than a puma.
5. **Snake encounter: freeze, then back away.** Two slow steps back; it doesn't want to waste venom on something it can't eat. NEVER touch it "to move it", not even dead (bite reflex).
6. **Swarming wasps/bees: run straight and far.** At least 200-300 m, through brush if you can; do NOT jump into water (they wait for you). Cover face and neck.
7. **For a serious bite or sting**, switch to the bites & stings guide: immobilize, note the time, and NO tourniquets or sucking venom.

## What NOT to do

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct |
|-----------|-----------|
| Reaching hands/feet where you can't see | Look first, use a stick to move things |
| Sleeping directly on leaf-littered ground | Raised bed of branches + inspect the area |
| Approaching "abandoned" baby animals | The mother is close: leave NOW |
| Touching hairy caterpillars, colorful frogs or milky-sap plants | Bright colors = chemical warning |
| Panic-running through brush | Walk firmly: falls are accident #1 |
| Hanging food next to where you sleep | Food 50+ m away and raised: don't invite animals to bed |

## The real dangers, in order

1. **Falls and sprains** — the true #1 accident in the wild.
2. **Falling dead branches** — ALWAYS check overhead.
3. **Insects** (wasps, ants, ticks, mosquitoes) — more hospitalizations than all mammals combined.
4. **Snakes** — avoidable by watching your step.
5. **Stinging plants and thorns** — infection if not cleaned.
6. **Large mammals** — last on the list: they avoid you.

## Ticks and leeches

- Tick: remove with tweezers by the HEAD, slow straight pull. Don't burn it or drown it in alcohol (it regurgitates).
- Leech: slide a fingernail under its mouth to detach; don't rip it off.
- Note the bite date: fever in the following days = medical care.

## Example: Forest Dangers

**Situation:** Dusk falls, you're gathering firewood and there's deep leaf litter between fallen logs.

**Do:** 1. Use a long stick to stir the litter before reaching in. 2. Step on each log and look at the far side before stepping down. 3. Gather wood making normal noise. 4. Before sleeping, shake out boots and check your shelter's ceiling.

**Avoid:** Don't gather wood at night, don't reach into holes, don't touch nests or hives, don't keep food in your shelter.

**Escalate:** If a snake bites or a swarm attacks with multiple stings (or allergy signs: swollen face, wheezing), switch NOW to the bites/stings guide and trigger the mesh SOS.
