# Bites and stings

> ⚠️ This guide does not replace professional medical care. Get medical help as soon as possible.

## SNAKE

### What NOT to do (myths kill more than venom)

- ❌ DON'T cut the wound, DON'T suck the venom, NO tourniquet, NO ice, NO electric shocks, NO alcohol or coffee.
- ❌ DON'T try to catch or kill the snake (second bite). A distant PHOTO is enough; otherwise memorize colors and shape.

### Steps

1. **Move the person away from the snake.** Sit them down. CALM: panic speeds the heart and spreads venom.
2. **Remove rings, bracelets, shoes** from the bitten limb BEFORE it swells.
3. **Splint the limb** softly, keep it BELOW heart level. Zero walking if it's a leg (carry them).
4. **Mark the swelling edge with a pen and the time**; repeat every 15 min (shows the doctor how fast the venom moves).
5. Wash gently with soap and water. Loose gauze cover.
6. **Urgent transport to a hospital with antivenom** (call ahead if possible). Antivenom is the ONLY real treatment.
7. Even if "painless" or a dry bite: minimum 6-8 h observation.

## DOG / CAT / HUMAN / BAT

1. **Wash NOW with plenty of soap and running water for 10-15 minutes**, into the wound — THE single most important anti-rabies, anti-infection measure.
2. Control bleeding with pressure. Cover with gauze.
3. **ALWAYS see a doctor the same day:** most need antibiotics (especially cat and human bites) plus **rabies** evaluation (vaccine) and tetanus. Bat contact = rabies until proven otherwise, even with no visible mark.
4. Owned animal: get the owner's details and vaccine status; observe the animal 10 days.

## SCORPION

1. Wash, cloth-wrapped cold, over-the-counter painkiller per label.
2. **Small children, or symptoms beyond local pain** (drooling, heavy sweating, blurred vision, odd eye/tongue movements, trouble breathing) → hospital URGENTLY (may need antivenom).
3. Adult with only local pain: watch 4-6 hours.

## BEE / WASP

1. **Bee stinger: scrape it out NOW** with a card edge or fingernail (don't squeeze with tweezers: it injects more). Every second counts.
2. Wash; wrapped cold 10-15 min; painkiller and/or antihistamine per label.
3. Many stings at once (swarm), or a sting inside mouth/throat → doctor urgently.

## 🚨 ANAPHYLAXIS (severe allergy — possible after ANY sting, food or medicine)

**Suspect it:** within minutes — hives all over, swelling lips/tongue/eyelids, wheeze or trouble breathing, tight throat, hoarse voice, vomiting, dizziness or collapse.

1. **Epinephrine auto-injector if available (EpiPen): USE IT NOW** on the outer thigh, through clothing if needed. Don't hesitate: waiting kills, the shot doesn't.
2. **Urgent transport ALWAYS**, even if they improve (it can rebound in 4-8 h).
3. Lie them down, legs raised (breathing badly: semi-sitting; pregnant: left side).
4. Oral antihistamine helps hives but does NOT replace epinephrine or the hospital.
5. Breathing stops → CPR.

## Signs of deterioration (all bites)

- Fast-spreading swelling, whole-body hives, wheezing → treat as anaphylaxis.
- Fever, pus, growing pain days later → infection: see a doctor.

## Example practice: Bites and stings (snake, dog, scorpion, bee)

**Situation:** On a farm, someone feels sudden pain after a bite or sting and is unsure what animal caused it.

**Do:** 1. Make the scene safe and assign one specific person to call emergency services. 2. Follow the large steps in this guide in order instead of jumping to the most dramatic action. 3. Use gloves, barriers, or clean cloth if available. 4. Recheck every minute: breathing, responsiveness, bleeding, temperature, or worsening signs.

**Avoid:** Do not improvise advanced procedures, do not move the neck/spine if injury is suspected, do not give food or drink to a confused person, and do not delay calling because you are searching for perfect equipment.

**Escalate:** Call or evacuate immediately if breathing is not normal, chest pain appears, bleeding does not stop, confusion worsens, a burn is large, poisoning is possible, pregnancy is advanced, or there are multiple victims.

**Quick check:** before closing the guide, say out loud what happened, what you did, what is missing, and who is responsible next. This is a guided practice example; adapt it to your environment and prioritize professional help when available.
